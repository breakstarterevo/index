November Stock Report: Chelsea, Madrid And Brighton Buy Separation

Chelsea, Real Madrid and Brighton rose with force in November, while Crystal Palace, Valencia and Juventus made their problems impossible to hide.

Damon CrossNovember 1982

Stop pretending every early result needs patience. Some teams made November clear. Some teams made it loud. Some teams made it a problem.

The stock report is not just a standings sort. It is about what changed, why it matters, and whether the next month should feel like opportunity or repair.

Stock Up: Chelsea

Chelsea went 7-1, closed on a W6, and put a plus-98 margin on Tier 2. That is not a nice start. That is a promotion statement.

The 109-76 win over Monaco showed the ceiling. The one-point escape against Juventus showed the usefulness. Jack Sikma's group won blowouts and survived discomfort, which is exactly how a team starts turning the table into a weapon.

Stock Up: Real Madrid

Real Madrid went 6-1 with a plus-108 margin in the hardest tier. They beat AC Milan by 25, then by 32, and also took down AFC Richmond. That is the resume. No extra decoration needed.

Sidney Moncrief gives Madrid the kind of player who makes the whole thing feel adult. The Atletico Madrid loss is a reminder, not a rebuttal. Madrid answered it by winning three straight.

Stock Up: Brighton

Brighton are 5-2 with a plus-90 margin. That is the quietest loud month in the league. Chelsea have the better record, but Brighton's average margin says they are not simply hanging around.

Kiki Vandeweghe gives them scoring gravity, and the 135-100 win over Monaco made the promotion case easy to see. Brighton are not chasing relevance. They are defending a promotion place.

Stock Down: Crystal Palace

Crystal Palace went 1-6, finished on an L4, and own a minus-94 margin. That is not a rough patch. That is a warning label.

The 143-95 loss at AC Milan will be the number everyone remembers, but the larger issue is that Palace have not found a version of themselves that travels from game to game. Darrell Griffith can give them points. He cannot personally patch a 48-point hole.

Stock Down: Valencia

Valencia's month had one good breath: a 112-102 win at Tottenham Hotspur. Then came the 108-66 loss at Inter Milan, and the room changed.

Isiah Thomas is still the reason to keep watching. The team around him is the reason this section exists. A 1-5 month and minus-66 margin leaves Valencia at the bottom of a tier where the top is already moving.

Stock Down: Juventus

Juventus are the most annoying kind of down stock: talented enough to make the argument, inconsistent enough to lose it. They beat Ajax by 30, then lost to Ajax by 23. They pushed Chelsea to one point, but it still counted as a loss.

Kareem Abdul-Jabbar gives them credibility. The 1-5 record gives them the line. At some point, close and interesting has to become winning.

November did not ask for patience. It asked teams to show who had control, and only a few answered properly.

Chelsea, Madrid and Brighton leave the month with leverage. Palace, Valencia and Juventus leave it with work. That is the whole stock board.
