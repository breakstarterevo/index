January Tier 2 Power Rankings: Chelsea Lead, Juventus Reappear

Chelsea keep the Tier 2 top line, but Juventus' 6-1 month changes the promotion chase and puts pressure on Marseille and FL Fart.

Nina ValeJanuary 1983

Tier 2 has a stable leader and an unstable chase. Chelsea still have the best season profile, but Juventus authored the month that forces the board to move.

1. Chelsea - stayed level at No. 1

Chelsea are 16-7 overall with a plus-200 differential, still the cleanest promotion case in the tier. A 5-3 January was not spotless, but Jack Sikma's team closed W3 and beat FL Fart by 26. The top spot remains earned.

2. Juventus - up six spots

This is the obvious climb. Juventus went 6-1, finished W5 and beat FL Fart by 20. Kareem Abdul-Jabbar gives them a reliable late-clock answer, but the bigger change is that the table now sees Juventus as promotion-live rather than relegation-adjacent.

3. Marseille - stayed level

Marseille remain second overall at 12-10, but a 4-3 month with a minus-36 differential leaves the resume vulnerable. Robert Parish still anchors the best argument. The 95-94 win over Chelsea matters because close wins against the leader travel well.

4. FL Fart - stayed level

FL Fart sit 11-10 overall, close enough to matter and uneven enough to doubt. Larry Drew's team beat Ajax by 28, then lost twice to close January. Fourth is fair because the chase is crowded, not because the form is clean.

5. Brighton - down three spots

Brighton's season margin remains excellent at plus-132, and Kiki Vandeweghe still gives the attack a premium scorer. January was a problem: 2-4, L2, and an 18-point loss at Manchester United. The old profile keeps them above the bottom group; the month knocks them down.

6. Manchester United - stayed level

United were 2-4 in January, which is not enough to rise, but they beat Brighton by 18 and Chelsea by seven. Walter Davis and Jeff Ruland give them enough quality to be more than a relegation note. The consistency has not followed.

7. Ajax - down two spots

Ajax went 3-3, a perfectly acceptable month that still leaves them 8-12 overall with a minus-110 differential. Cliff Robinson gives them a future-facing case, but the present board needs more proof.

8. Monaco - down one spot

Monaco's 2-5 January and L5 finish put them last. Danny Ainge's team beat Juventus early in the month, then lost the plot. A 9-14 overall record and minus-106 differential make this more than a temporary dip.

Chelsea still own the tier, but Juventus are the team that made the table recalculate itself.

The next board will depend on whether Juventus sustain the surge and whether Brighton's season-long margin turns back into wins.
