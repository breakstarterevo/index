November 1984 Review: Richmond Set The Temperature

Richmond imposed order on Tier 1, PSG made promotion look practical, and Tier 3 answered both with the league's most crowded race.

Graham TrentNovember 1984

By the end of November, AFC Richmond's opponents were no longer trying to solve a new season. They were trying to survive its most settled team. Richmond went 6-1, won five straight to close and outscored the month by 144 points. The single loss, by three to FL Fart, now looks less like a crack than a useful reminder that even authority can misplace an evening.

Richmond Made Tier 1 Unequal

The 136-103 win over AC Milan was the clearest statement because Milan had enough size and scoring to make the matchup informative. Richmond still separated. Michael Cooper gives the roster its defensive grammar; Tom Chambers supplied an outrageous scoring efficiency inside it. Their partnership matters more than either headline number because one creates the possessions the other ends.

Milan recovered by beating Benfica 144-97, the month's largest margin. It was both impressive and inconvenient: the same club can look like Richmond's nearest challenger and then look thirty-three points short of the job.

PSG Built The Cleanest Promotion Case

Paris Saint-Germain's 5-1 month included wins by 43, 37 and 32. Kelly Tripucka has turned efficient volume scoring into the visible engine, but the schedule also found PSG's vulnerability. Aston Villa won 135-131 in Paris, then closed on three straight wins to sit half a game behind Real Madrid's second promotion place.

Marseille lost the month's closest game, 147-146 at Atletico, and remain only one game from the line. That single point may eventually become important; for now it is simply evidence that Tier 2 has four clubs close enough to make every direct meeting expensive.

Tier 3 Refused A Simple Story

Barcelona lead at 6-2, yet Tottenham are half a game back and Manchester City, Inter Milan and Monaco are one game behind. Tottenham beat Barcelona late in the month. Inter closed on three consecutive wins and crushed Sporting 116-75. The only promotion place is currently hosting five credible arguments.

At the other end, Sporting and Manchester United are both 1-8. Sporting's lone November win came against United; United's came against City. Each can point to one result that proves possibility. Neither has produced a sequence that resembles progress.

The Result That Would Not Behave

Benfica's 125-90 win over Brighton was the month's sharpest upset. It did not launch a recovery. Benfica lost the next three and ended beneath the Tier 1 relegation line. The game therefore survives as a warning about Brighton and a mystery about Benfica: how can a team capable of that much control reproduce so little of it?

November's winners did more than collect victories. They made the league adjust its expectations.

December begins with Richmond and PSG defending separation, Barcelona defending a line without distance, and Chelsea trying to prove that a positive margin can still rescue a losing record. The first month gave the season shape. The next one decides which shapes hold.
