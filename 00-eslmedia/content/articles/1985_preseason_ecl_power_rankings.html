1985 ECL Preseason Power Rankings: Manchester City Turned Promise Into Pressure

City produced the preseason's widest margin, but Barcelona's star pairing and Valencia's improved frontcourt make a one-promotion tier feel crowded before opening night.

Nina Vale1985 Preseason - ECL

ECL permits only one promotion, so a promising preseason is useful only if it identifies a sustainable advantage. Manchester City found several. Barcelona found two elite scorers. Valencia found a frontcourt. The rest of the tier begins with less certainty and no second promotion place to soften the cost of delay.

The ranking lens rewards scoring margin, defensive stability and roles that should survive regular-season scouting. Records break ties; they do not end the discussion. City leads because it dominated without depending on one impossible percentage or one player absorbing the entire offence.

1Manchester CityPRESEASON ENTRY
5-1 · +18.7 margin · Mike Dunleavy 18.7 PPG · Eddy Curry 10.2 RPG

City won through distribution rather than spectacle. Dunleavy led the scoring at only 18.7 points, which means the league's best average margin emerged without a star carrying an unsustainable load. Curry controlled the boards, Luol Deng supplied 12.7 points, 6.7 rebounds and three assists, and the defence allowed just 92.3 points per game. The concern is the same fact viewed from the other side: when a regular-season game requires one player to create a difficult basket, City has not yet identified its closer.

2BarcelonaPRESEASON ENTRY
5-1 · +5.8 margin · Bernard King 35.7 PPG · Darnell Valentine 11.8 APG

King and Paul Pierce combined for 62.9 points per game, and Pierce's camp rise survived first contact with live defence. Jalen Duren added nine rebounds while Valentine supplied the ball. Barcelona's issue is not creation; it is a 114.5-point defensive allowance that made a five-win run more laborious than City's. In a one-promotion tier, Barcelona cannot rely on winning every scoring contest simply because it owns the two best scorers in most of them.

3ValenciaPRESEASON ENTRY
4-2 · +6.8 margin · Otis Thorpe 26.0 PPG · Isiah Thomas 10.2 APG

Thorpe shot 71.4 percent, a number that will decline and a role that should not. Pervis Ellison added 20.3 points and disruptive defence, giving Thomas two interior partners capable of finishing what he creates. Valencia ranks below Barcelona because its perimeter scoring remains easier to crowd. It ranks above the rest because the ball already knows where to go and the frontcourt can survive imperfect shooting nights.

4MonacoPRESEASON ENTRY
4-2 · +1.7 margin · Bilal Coulibaly 17.3 PPG · Andre Drummond 12.7 RPG

Monaco won four games without controlling them. Drummond translated his camp leap into 17.0 points, 12.7 rebounds and 3.2 combined steals and blocks, while Alvin Robertson averaged 8.8 assists. Coulibaly led a balanced scoring group. The narrow margin keeps Monaco fourth: close games can indicate useful composure or a failure to separate. The regular season will decide which description fits.

5Sporting CPPRESEASON ENTRY
4-2 · +1.2 margin · Jim Jackson 22.3 PPG · Trayce Jackson-Davis 10.5 RPG

Sporting defended well enough to keep its games at a manageable 101.0-point pace and received a credible lead performance from Jackson. Trayce Jackson-Davis supplied the interior work. What it did not show was an easy source of secondary creation; Jackson also led the club with 4.2 assists. Sporting can make opponents uncomfortable. To climb, it must make its own half-court possessions less uncomfortable.

6Tottenham HotspurPRESEASON ENTRY
1-5 · -8.2 margin · Hubert Davis 22.5 PPG · Mike Conley 7.8 APG

Tottenham sits above two teams with equally difficult results because Davis and Conley at least supplied a plausible perimeter structure. Kurt Thomas averaged 8.8 rebounds, but the club scored only 102.5 per game and rarely generated pressure beyond the first option. Davis' eight-point camp gain was real. Tottenham now needs a second advantage before opponents start treating him as the entire scouting report.

7JuventusPRESEASON ENTRY
0-6 · -12.2 margin · Dirk Nowitzki 23.8 PPG, 8.3 RPG · Thaddeus Young 4.0 APG

Nowitzki gave Juventus scoring and rebounding, but the club failed to build enough playmaking around him. A team assist leader at four per game is a structural alarm. Juventus ranks seventh rather than eighth because its average margin was slightly less damaging and Nowitzki's role remains scalable. The club's next move must make his shooting create opportunities for others instead of merely decorating a stalled possession.

8Manchester UnitedPRESEASON ENTRY
1-5 · -13.8 margin · J.J. Redick 24.3 PPG · Tony Parker 7.5 APG

Redick shot well and Parker moved the ball, yet United produced the tier's lowest scoring average at 96.5 while allowing 110.3. The roster has two useful offensive functions—movement shooting and dribble creation—but not enough connective play between them. United's single win prevents a clean sweep of misery, not the bottom ranking. Until its bigs convert perimeter advantages into paint control, every possession will ask the guards to begin again.

One Place Changes The Calculation

With one promotion place, being the fourth-most convincing team has little strategic value. Monaco and Sporting cannot settle for respectable four-win preseasons if City, Barcelona and Valencia have already found stronger identities. They need an advantage that survives direct meetings: Drummond controlling the paint for Monaco, or Sporting turning its defensive pace into enough easy offence to escape half-court strain.

City's advantage is depth of function. No scorer averaged 20, yet the club won by nearly 19 per game. Barcelona's advantage is concentrated star force. Valencia's lies in the Thomas-Thorpe-Ellison triangle. These are distinct arguments, which makes the opening month informative rather than ceremonial. The club that imposes its preferred kind of game will own the promotion conversation.

The bottom two cannot wait for that conversation to develop around them. Juventus needs a creator who can use Nowitzki's gravity. United needs interior production that prevents Redick and Parker from restarting every possession. A one-promotion tier offers no reward for a late aesthetic improvement. By the time those solutions become comfortable, the top three may already be measuring distance.

City's lead is significant and still provisional. Barcelona can erase it by defending at an ordinary level. Valencia can narrow it if Thorpe and Ellison keep giving Thomas a reliable interior destination. ECL has only one route upward, which means the next board will care less about who looked promising and more about who established an advantage the rest of the tier cannot copy.
