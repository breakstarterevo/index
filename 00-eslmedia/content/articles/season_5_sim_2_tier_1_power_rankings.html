December 1985 Tier 1 Power Rankings: Richmond Keep The Room

The leader is unchanged, the chase has narrowed to two convincing profiles, and the bottom now punishes anyone trying to survive on isolated good nights.

Nina ValeDecember 1985

This board begins with the full-season file, uses December to measure direction and treats the two relegation places as a test of how much risk each weakness carries. That method produces little drama at No. 1 and considerable discomfort everywhere else.

AFC Richmond remain the most trustworthy Tier 1 team. Benfica and Paris Saint-Germain have become credible challengers rather than interesting alternatives. Beneath them, Real Madrid's winning streak is trying to outrun a modest scoring margin, while three bottom clubs have run out of room for theoretical improvement.

Tier 1 Trust Board — December 1985
  1. 1AFC RichmondSTAYED NO. 1
    12-4 overall | 5-2 December | +141 | W1 | Michael Cooper

    Richmond lost by one to Crystal Palace and by 14 to PSG, which is enough evidence to reject invincibility. It is not enough to reject control. They still won the month by 69 points, corrected AC Milan 99-78 and own the tier's best record and margin. The concern is that two challengers now know the door opens; the next trigger is whether Richmond close it in their direct meetings.

  2. 2BenficaSTAYED NO. 2
    11-6 | 5-3 December | +91 | L1 | Moses Malone

    Moses Malone gives Benfica the division's most dependable interior argument: 31.9 points, 11.2 rebounds and 3.6 blocks. December added five wins and a 32-point demolition of Milan. Three close games and the closing loss keep them below Richmond, but Benfica are no longer rising on projection. They are 1.5 games back with a profile built to stay there.

  3. 3Paris Saint-GermainSTAYED NO. 3
    9-6 | 5-2 December | +98 | W2 | Kelly Tripucka

    PSG made the strongest case for movement and remain third only because Benfica have two additional wins. The December margin was plus-63, Chelsea lost in Paris by 38 and Richmond lost there by 14. Kelly Tripucka supplies high-volume scoring; the 2-4 road record is the clean objection. If that travels in January, the top two becomes a top three.

  4. 4Real MadridSTAYED NO. 4
    9-7 | 5-4 December | +23 | W5 | Sidney Moncrief

    Five consecutive wins deserve trust, especially with Sidney Moncrief providing 31.3 points and real two-way pressure. The season margin remains only plus-23, and December included four losses before the streak took hold. Real's current form is better than fourth; its total body of work is not yet stronger than PSG's.

  5. 5BrightonSTAYED NO. 5
    7-8 | 3-5 December | +21 | W1 | Kiki Vandeweghe

    Kiki Vandeweghe is averaging 38.7 points on 70.2 percent true shooting, a sentence that sounds like it should support more than seven wins. Brighton remain positive on scoring margin and dominant at home. They are 1-7 on the road. The ranking stays because the underlying profile is better than the record; the concern is that travel has become structure, not variance.

  6. 6AC MilanUP 1 FROM NO. 7
    6-11 | 3-4 December | -126 | L2 | Joe Barry Carroll

    Milan move above Palace by half a game and little else. Joe Barry Carroll's 35 points and 3.7 blocks keep producing a star-level case inside a team that loses its minutes by 7.4 points a night. A win over Benfica shows the ceiling. Losses by 21 at Richmond and 32 at Benfica show how quickly the floor reappears.

  7. 7Crystal PalaceUP 1 FROM NO. 8
    5-11 | 3-5 December | -75 | L1 | Darrell Griffith

    The one-point win at Richmond is the best result owned by any bottom-three team. Palace also played six close games in December, evidence that they are more competitive than the record's shape suggests. Close is not safe. At 5-11 and on the relegation line, Alex English and Darrell Griffith need those final possessions to stop becoming compliments.

  8. 8ChelseaDOWN 2 FROM NO. 6
    5-11 | 1-5 December | -173 | L2 | Jim Paxson

    Chelsea had no close games in December. They beat Brighton by nine and lost the other five by a combined 115. That is not a team failing at the margins; it is a team repeatedly removed from them. Jim Paxson can score. The defense has made his work an opening statement in games that stop listening.

Board Notes
  • Control team: AFC Richmond, because record and margin still agree.
  • Biggest pressure: PSG's road form; the home evidence already belongs near the top.
  • Trust problem: Brighton away from home.
  • Panic team: Chelsea, with one December win and no close-loss cushion.

The next board changes if PSG travel, Real's streak survives stronger opposition or one of the bottom three produces an actual run. Until then, Richmond keep the room and everyone else is arguing over distance from the door.
