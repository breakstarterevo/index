January 1984 Tier 1 Power Rankings: Richmond Stay First, Benfica Close Hard

Richmond still own the strongest full-season profile, but January made the chase more credible and the bottom of Tier 1 less forgiving.

Nina ValeSeason 3 - Sim 3 power board

Power rankings are not standings, but they cannot ignore the table. Tier 1 still starts with AFC Richmond because 19-3, a plus-260 season differential and a W4 close are too much evidence to throw out after one upset loss. The more interesting January development is below them: Benfica looked like the best current challenger, while the danger group stayed crowded enough that one clean sim can change survival math.

Tier 1 Board - January 1984
  1. 1AFC RichmondStayed No. 1
    19-3 overall | January 5-2 | +11.8 season margin | W4 | Star context: Michael Cooper
    Richmond absorbed the Bayern upset without losing the board because the whole-season profile still clears the tier. Their worst January result was a four-point loss; their answer was a four-game win streak. That is not invincibility, but it is still control.
    ReasonBest record, best margin, and no extended wobble after the loss.
    ConcernThe leader no longer feels untouchable against high-end shot creation.
    Next triggerAnother contender loss would turn this from a reset into a real chase.
  2. 2BenficaUp 1
    15-9 overall | January 6-2 | +11.5 January margin | W1 | Star context: Moses Malone
    Benfica used January the way a contender is supposed to use it: by turning a good roster into a cleaner argument. Moses Malone gives them the interior floor, and Adrian Dantley gives the offense enough half-court certainty to make the 6-2 month feel sustainable.
    ReasonBest current challenger form with a plus-92 January differential.
    ConcernStill five games behind Richmond, so this is a trust rise, not a standings takeover.
    Next triggerA direct win over a top-three Tier 1 team would make the chase louder.
  3. 3AC MilanDown 1
    14-10 overall | January 4-3 | +3.0 January margin | L1 | Star context: Larry Bird
    Milan remain dangerous because Larry Bird keeps the offense serious and the season-long differential is still positive. The drop is about force, not collapse. A 4-3 January was respectable, but Benfica's month had more bite.
    ReasonPositive full-season profile and enough star power to beat anyone.
    ConcernJanuary did not separate them from the pack.
    Next triggerNeed a cleaner scoring month to reclaim No. 2.
  4. 4ChelseaStayed No. 4
    10-10 overall | January 4-4 | -4.8 January margin | W2 | Star context: Jack Sikma
    Chelsea are the hinge team. The record is level, the January margin is ugly, and the W2 streak keeps them from slipping into the fear tier. They are not close to the title argument, but they are far enough above the drop line to control their own mood.
    ReasonTwo-game closing streak and a better table position than the danger group.
    ConcernNegative margin says the record is flattering the month.
    Next triggerTurn W2 into a real buffer before Real or Bayern recover.
  5. 5FL FartStayed No. 5
    10-13 overall | January 2-4 | -11.3 January margin | W2 | Star context: Larry Drew
    FL Fart's W2 finish matters, but the rest of January does not inspire trust. A team can be above the relegation spots and still look shaky; that is where FL Fart sit, with enough offense to survive and enough defensive damage to keep the door open.
    ReasonStill above the deepest danger and closed the sim with wins.
    ConcernMinus-68 in the latest window is a survival warning.
    Next triggerNeed a positive-margin sim, not just a split record.
  6. 6Bayern MunichUp 2
    8-15 overall | January 4-4 | -1.4 January margin | L2 | Star context: Bernard King
    Bayern are still in a relegation spot by the standings, so this is not comfort. The rise is about the quality of the signal: beating Richmond proves the ceiling is higher than the record, and Bernard King gives them a route out if the supporting results stabilize.
    ReasonRichmond upset plus a competitive January profile.
    ConcernStill 8-15 and still on the actual drop line.
    Next triggerClimb above Real Madrid or Sheffield in the table, not just the trust board.
  7. 7Real MadridDown 1
    8-15 overall | January 2-6 | -1.9 January margin | L3 | Star context: Sidney Moncrief
    Real Madrid have enough individual quality to make the bottom feel strange, but the board cannot keep paying for name value. The L3 finish is the problem. They are not buried, but the direction is wrong.
    ReasonMoncrief keeps the floor from collapsing completely.
    ConcernThe losing streak is arriving exactly when the bottom is tightening.
    Next triggerStop the slide before Bayern's upset win becomes the bigger survival story.
  8. 8Sheffield UnitedDown 1
    7-16 overall | January 2-4 | -4.2 January margin | W1 | Star context: Rickey Green
    Sheffield are not hopeless, but eighth is fair because the standings and the margin agree. The W1 finish gives them a little oxygen. It does not change the fact that they have the worst record in the tier and need a full correction, not a useful night.
    ReasonThe last win keeps the season from feeling abandoned.
    ConcernWorst Tier 1 record and the weakest season differential.
    Next triggerA winning sim is required to leave the bottom of the board.
Board Notes
  • Control team: AFC Richmond, because the season-long record still outweighs the January blemish.
  • Biggest riser: Benfica, whose plus-92 January gives the chase real substance.
  • Trust problem: Real Madrid, where the roster reads better than the direction.
  • Panic team: Sheffield United, because the standings and margin both point down.

The next board changes if Benfica turn this chase into direct pressure or if Bayern convert the Richmond upset into table movement. Until then, Richmond remain first and everyone else is arguing for proximity.
