November 1985 MVP Race: Moses Malone Makes Winning The Tiebreaker

The league's most outrageous stat lines belong to teams all over the table, forcing the first ballot to decide when production becomes value.

Damon CrossNovember 1985

Joe Barry Carroll is averaging 39.6 points and 4.2 blocks. Kiki Vandeweghe is scoring 41.1 on efficiency that looks like a typo. Steve Johnson is giving AC Sparta Praha 35 and 11. If this ballot were a box-score beauty contest, we could finish before lunch.

It is not. The availability line is sixty percent of team games. Production opens the door; efficiency, two-way influence and the degree to which that production changes a team's season decide who walks through it. November's first ballot is where losing becomes an actual cost.

Tier 1 Ballot
The leader: Moses Malone
  1. 1Moses Malone, Benfica
    33.9 PTS | 11.4 REB | 2.9 BLK | 60.8 TS%
    Malone owns the complete argument. He scores at elite volume, controls the glass, protects the rim and does it for a 6-3 team sitting one game from first. No apology is required.
  2. 2Kiki Vandeweghe, Brighton
    41.1 PTS | 4.4 REB | 73.8 TS% | +2.1
    Forty-one points on this efficiency is ridiculous. Brighton's 4-3 record and nearly level margin are not. Kiki has the strongest offensive line and a team context too ordinary to ignore.
  3. 3Joe Barry Carroll, AC Milan
    39.6 PTS | 7.3 REB | 4.2 BLK | 64.1 TS%
    Carroll's two-way production may be the ballot's most difficult line to reproduce. Milan are 3-7. At some point value must appear outside the player's row, even when the team's failures are hardly his alone.
  4. 4Sidney Moncrief, Real Madrid
    32.6 PTS | 6.0 REB | 4.1 AST | 2.0 STL
    Moncrief gives a 4-3 team scoring, physical defense and late-game control. The modest 57.6 true-shooting mark keeps him below the three overwhelming production cases.
  5. 5Tom Chambers, AFC Richmond
    28.4 PTS | 6.6 REB | 63.1 TS% | 7-2 team
    Richmond's balance divides credit, but Chambers remains its clearest scoring constant. He is fifth because the leader deserves representation—not first because Richmond can win without asking him for forty.
Tier 2 Ballot
The leader: George Gervin
  1. 1George Gervin, Inter Milan
    27.9 PTS | 69.5 TS% | +10.0 | 6-1 team
    Gervin turns possessions into points without wasting either, and Inter own the tier's best record and margin. His volume is lower than Johnson's or Nance's; his impact arrives with fewer repair costs.
  2. 2Andre Iguodala, Ajax
    29.8 PTS | 4.9 REB | 2.4 STL | 71.7 TS%
    Iguodala has the ballot's most efficient high-volume line and a 6-2 team behind it. Gervin wins the first tiebreaker because Inter sit above Ajax. The gap is almost ceremonial.
  3. 3Larry Drew, FL Fart
    25.0 PTS | 10.9 AST | 1.9 STL | +7.2
    Drew organizes the division's highest-win team and creates nearly eleven assists a night. The 54.5 true-shooting mark is the tax attached to that enormous playmaking burden.
  4. 4Steve Johnson, AC Sparta Praha
    35.1 PTS | 10.9 REB | 66.4 TS% | +2.2
    Johnson's preseason explosion survived official basketball. AC Sparta Praha's 4-5 record survived with it. This is an MVP stat line carrying a team that has not yet converted brilliance into a winning season.
  5. 5Larry Nance, Aston Villa
    32.2 PTS | 10.7 REB | 2.7 BLK | 63.6 TS%
    Nance scores, rebounds and protects the rim at star level. Villa are also 4-5 with a negative margin. He and Johnson are separated less by performance than by how much losing one ballot can hold.
Tier 3 Ballot
The leader: Bernard King
  1. 1Bernard King, Barcelona
    27.8 PTS | 6.0 REB | 67.7 TS% | +7.0
    King is the most efficient leading scorer on the 6-2 promotion favorite. Barcelona have other stars; he is the cleanest reason their offense turns close games into separation.
  2. 2Isiah Thomas, Valencia
    21.9 PTS | 12.4 AST | 2.5 STL | +7.9
    Thomas controls more of Valencia's geometry than any scorer controls his own column. The shooting is uneven. Twelve assists and a one-game promotion gap make that a manageable imperfection.
  3. 3Steve Kerr, Tottenham Hotspur
    29.1 PTS | 4.3 AST | 2.0 STL | 69.9 TS%
    Kerr's shooting bends defenses before the action begins and helped Tottenham hand Barcelona their clearest loss. An even team record keeps the case behind the line leaders.
  4. 4Chris Bosh, Arsenal
    30.4 PTS | 10.2 REB | 1.6 BLK | 69.3 TS%
    Bosh owns the strongest individual line in the tier. Sporting own three wins. He is fourth not because the production needs help, but because his team plainly does.
  5. 5Andre Drummond, Monaco
    26.1 PTS | 14.7 REB | 2.3 BLK | 9 games
    Nobody controls more missed shots. Drummond's free-throw problem and Monaco's negative margin make the dominance less portable late in games.
Carroll and Bosh have numbers that demand attention. Malone, Gervin and King have numbers that already changed the top of a table.

December will test the ballot's first principle. If Milan climb, Carroll can erase the context penalty quickly. If Brighton keep hovering near level, Vandeweghe's scoring becomes louder and less decisive at the same time. The stars have done enough. Now their teams get a vote.
