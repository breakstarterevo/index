December 1984 Tier 2 Power Rankings: Real Madrid Break The Board Open

PSG did almost nothing wrong, but Real Madrid's unbeaten month changed first place from a debate into an obligation.

Nina ValeDecember 1984

Real Madrid won eight December games by 165 points. Their closest result was a nine-point win. A board that left them second because Paris Saint-Germain remained excellent would not be patient; it would be inattentive.

The promotion line is now held by two teams with a combined 25-5 record and plus-522 differential. The more revealing movement happens below them, where Ajax have joined the credible class and Aston Villa have fallen through what looked like a sturdy November floor.

Tier 2 Trust BoardDecember 1984
  1. 1Real MadridUP 1 FROM NO. 2
    13-2 overall | 8-0 December | +251 | W8 | Sidney Moncrief

    The numbers are excessive in useful ways: 20.6 points per game better than opponents in December, no single-digit scares until a nine-point win and Sidney Moncrief scoring 27.7 on 66.7 percent true shooting. Real have stopped asking for trust and started collecting it.

  2. 2Paris Saint-GermainDOWN 1 FROM NO. 1
    12-3 | 6-2 December | +271 | W3 | Kelly Tripucka

    PSG own the tier's best full-season differential and Kelly Tripucka's 33.3 points per game come with a plus-14.3 individual margin. They fall because Real's month demanded it, not because the original case dissolved.

  3. 3AjaxUP 2 FROM NO. 5
    10-8 | 6-3 December | +24 | L1 | Andre Iguodala

    Ajax were five wins behind the promotion line entering the month and finished with six December victories and a positive season differential. The final loss prevents a cleaner close; the preceding evidence puts them alone in the second tier of Tier 2.

  4. 4Atletico MadridUP 2 FROM NO. 6
    7-9 | 3-3 December | -53 | L1 | Dennis Johnson

    A level month and a small positive December margin are modest achievements. In this section of the board, modest is enough. Johnny Moore's 14.1 assists and 3.3 steals give Atletico a repeatable organizing principle that the clubs below lack.

  5. 5MarseilleDOWN 1 FROM NO. 4
    7-9 | 3-6 December | -29 | L1 | Robert Parish

    Robert Parish gives Marseille 27.1 points and 13 rebounds, which makes a 3-6 month more frustrating rather than more defensible. They stay above the bottom three because the season margin is manageable and the center is real.

  6. 6JuventusUP 2 FROM NO. 8
    5-11 | 3-5 December | -121 | L1 | Gerald Henderson

    Juventus rise without pretending 3-5 is good. They were competitive enough to avoid the collapses consuming Villa and AC Sparta Praha, and that distinction matters in the relegation half of the board. It is survival evidence, not promotion evidence.

  7. 7Aston VillaDOWN 4 FROM NO. 3
    6-10 | 1-7 December | -135 | L7 | Larry Nance

    No club fell further. Larry Nance averaged 32.2 points, nine rebounds and 2.3 blocks while Villa lost seven straight and the month by 134. The star is not the excuse; the inability to turn his production into a competitive game is the indictment.

  8. 8AC Sparta PrahaDOWN 1 FROM NO. 7
    3-11 | 1-5 December | -208 | L2 | Bill Cartwright

    AC Sparta Praha's minus-208 differential is the tier's verdict. The lone December win interrupted the damage but did not alter it; four of the five losses came by at least seventeen. There is no ranking argument left below them.

Control Team: Real Madrid   Biggest Riser: Ajax   Best No. 2: PSG   Panic Team: Aston Villa

Real and PSG have made promotion look less like a race than a private agreement. Ajax are the only club currently equipped to interrupt it. Everyone else is deciding whether January is about climbing or merely avoiding AC Sparta Praha's end of the table.
