December 1984 Review: Real Madrid Stole The Month

Richmond retained the league's highest ground, but Real Madrid's perfect run changed promotion, power rankings and the meaning of a good December.

Graham TrentDecember 1984

December offered several respectable months and then placed Real Madrid beside them. Eight wins, no losses, plus-165. Their closest game was a nine-point victory, which is the kind of detail that makes ordinary competence look faintly impolite.

Real did not merely pass Paris Saint-Germain in Tier 2. They altered the scale by which the division is judged. PSG went 6-2, remained plus-271 for the season and continued to receive 33.3 efficient points from Kelly Tripucka. It was excellent work. Madrid were better every night.

Tier 1 Learned Richmond Can Bleed

AFC Richmond lost three December games after losing once across the opening month. Benfica beat them twice. Brighton found a two-point win. The expected conclusion—a newly open Tier 1—never arrived, because Richmond also won six games, finished plus-83 and closed on three straight victories.

The most vivid correction came against Crystal Palace: 134-89. Richmond's season differential is now plus-242. AC Milan remain second at 10-6 and plus-87, a perfectly credible profile that looks modest only because the leader has made proportion difficult.

The bottom is less orderly. Chelsea sit seventh at 7-11 despite a plus-48 differential. Benfica remain last despite a 4-3 month and two wins over Richmond. The standings have become an argument between outcomes and the quality of the minutes that produced them. Outcomes will decide relegation.

Tier 2 Split Into Three Different Seasons

Real and PSG are playing for control. Ajax are playing to force their way into that conversation after a 6-3 month. The remaining five clubs are playing increasingly different versions of survival.

Aston Villa supplied the month's hardest image: Larry Nance averaging 32.2 points, nine rebounds and 2.3 blocks while the team lost seven straight. AC Sparta Praha remain last with a minus-208 differential. Juventus improved just enough to separate resistance from recovery. None of it touched the promotion line.

Sidney Moncrief's 27.7 points on 66.7 percent true shooting gave Madrid's run a star who did not have to monopolize it. That may be the club's most durable advantage. The unbeaten month looked less like a player catching fire than an entire roster discovering there was no reason to cool down.

Tier 3 Refused To Choose One Truth

Barcelona lead at 11-5 and have won four straight. Inter Milan sit one game back with a much stronger margin than Tottenham. Tottenham sit half a game back with one fewer loss than Inter and a season differential of four.

All three can describe themselves as the most convincing promotion candidate if allowed to select the evidence. Barcelona own the actual place. Inter own the best underlying profile. Tottenham own a peculiar ability to win more games than their scoring margin recommends.

Paul Pierce remains the cleanest star case: 28.3 points, 67.1 percent true shooting and two steals for the leader. World B. Free gives Inter 25.6 points in thirty minutes. Tottenham's case is less easily assigned to one player, which may explain both its resilience and the suspicion surrounding it.

December did not close the league's races. It clarified which teams are entitled to define them.
What The Month Left Behind

The biggest win belonged to Richmond by 45 over Palace. The closest finish belonged to FL Fart by one over Brighton. The sharpest upset belonged to Benfica over Richmond. Aston Villa were the coldest team. Real Madrid were not merely the hottest; they were the team that made the adjective feel inadequate.

January begins with one clear league leader, two overwhelming Tier 2 promotion teams and three Tier 3 clubs arguing over a single place. December's gift was not certainty. It was a much better understanding of where uncertainty is allowed to live.
