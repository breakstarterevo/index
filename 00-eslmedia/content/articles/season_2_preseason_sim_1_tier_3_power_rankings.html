Season 2 Preseason Tier 3 Power Rankings: Villa Left A Mark

Aston Villa and Arsenal both open 5-1, but Villa's overwhelming scoring margin makes the first Tier 3 ranking unusually decisive.

Nina ValeSeason 2 - Preseason Sim 1

Aston Villa and Arsenal share the best preseason record in Tier 3. They do not share the same opening impression. Villa finished plus-96 across six games, a margin so pronounced that the first position requires little argument even before any official promotion contest begins.

The division still has plenty of uncertainty beneath its top two. Paris Saint-Germain played one additional game, Manchester City had genuine high-end moments, and Barcelona's 3-3 sequence managed to include both encouragement and concern.

1. Aston Villa - 5-1, plus-96

Villa did nearly everything a preseason leader can do. They beat Tottenham Hotspur twice, including a 122-87 dismantling, defeated Manchester City, and handled Paris Saint-Germain in both meetings. The sole loss at Barcelona keeps the result grounded. The broader evidence still says Villa were the league's most forceful side in Sim 1.

2. Arsenal - 5-1, plus-51

Sporting would lead most opening boards with this record and margin. They beat Paris Saint-Germain, Barcelona twice and Tottenham twice, with the only defeat coming in the PSG rematch. What separates them from Villa is not failure but dominance: Villa's differential was nearly twice as large.

3. Paris Saint-Germain - 4-3, plus-5

PSG played seven games and emerged just above level in margin, an honest indication of a competitive but unresolved side. Their road win at Arsenal is the result that holds this position. Two defeats to Villa demonstrate how far the first-place standard currently sits above them.

4. Manchester City - 3-3, plus-20

City are the strongest level-record team in the tier by point margin. Their 118-84 win over Barcelona supplied a dramatic example of their ceiling, while defeats to Villa prevent a leap into the leading group. If their better performances repeat, fourth will be temporary.

5. Barcelona - 3-3, minus-29

Barcelona beat Aston Villa, which immediately gives the new season a result worth remembering, and also recorded two wins over Valencia. Yet two heavy losses, to Manchester City and Arsenal, pulled their margin sharply negative. There is upside here; there is also an obvious need to make their bad nights survivable.

6. Valencia - 2-4, minus-8

Valencia were far closer to balanced than the record suggests. A win over Inter Milan and a one-point loss to Tottenham offer evidence of competitiveness, but the two defeats to Barcelona limit the case for a higher opening slot. They are within reach of the middle if they sharpen late-game results.

7. Inter Milan - 1-4, minus-43

Inter beat Manchester City once, a result that gives them a useful point of reference. The rest of their sim contained too much separation, including defeats to Valencia, PSG and Sporting. One credible win can begin a case; it cannot complete it.

8. Tottenham Hotspur - 1-5, minus-92

Tottenham escaped a winless sim by edging Valencia 96-95. That is the positive. The defining evidence remains a pair of heavy Villa defeats and a margin almost as severe as Villa's was impressive. Preseason grants time to repair; the opening picture gives them a clear reason to use it quickly.

Villa made a promotion-level impression in games that do not yet decide promotion. That distinction is the only reason the division still gets to breathe.

Arsenal have already made this a credible top-two debate. The remaining clubs must decide whether they are chasing that pair or preparing to punish them once the games begin to count.
