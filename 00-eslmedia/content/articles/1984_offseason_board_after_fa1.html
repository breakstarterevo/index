After FA1, Alex English Still Owns The Offseason Board

The first wave moved money, but it did not remove the market's best wing — or solve the scarcity that makes the next bids dangerous.

Nina Vale1984 Offseason - After FA1

An offseason board should measure more than raw talent. The useful question after FA1 is which available player can still force several clubs to rewrite their plans. By that standard, Alex English remains alone at the top.

English is 28, carries the strongest overall profile in the remaining pool, and fills the hardest role to find: a primary scoring wing who does not need a developmental excuse. Teams can debate price. They cannot debate the effect he would have on a rotation.

The Post-FA1 Top Ten
RankPlayerMarket case
  1. 1Alex English — SF, 28

    The one remaining player who can change a club's offensive hierarchy on arrival. Scarcity and certainty make him the board's clear market setter.

  2. 2Purvis Short — PF, 25

    Young enough to grow and already productive enough to justify a serious commitment. His 138-potential profile keeps the bidding ceiling high.

  3. 3Greg Ballard — SF, 27

    A premium wing in his prime. He sits behind English only because the first name on the board has the stronger takeover case.

  4. 4Kyle Macy — PG, 24

    The best blend of age, position and immediate control. Guard-needy clubs should see him as a long-term answer, not a fallback.

  5. 5Artis Gilmore — C, 32

    Age lowers the contract horizon, not the immediate value. Contenders seeking a dependable interior anchor have no cleaner option.

  6. 6James Donaldson — C, 24

    The younger big-man alternative. His age creates a wider bidder pool than Gilmore's, even if the immediate ceiling is lower.

  7. 7Rolando Blackman — SF, 23

    A young wing with starter-level value is exactly where patient teams should become aggressive.

  8. 8Dennis Johnson — SF, 27

    A strong prime-age two-way option whose 129-potential mark leaves room for more than a short-term patch.

  9. 9Rickey Green — PG, 27

    The guard market thins quickly after Macy. That makes Green's reliable lead-guard profile more valuable with every missed bid.

  10. 10Robert Reid — SF, 26

    Another credible prime-age wing in a market where several rosters still list wing depth as a clear need.

What Changes The Board

Price will reorder the middle, but fit should decide the next wave. Atletico Madrid and Aston Villa possess the room to shop broadly. Inter Milan has both spending power and needs across every position group. Crystal Palace has a narrower wing problem and enough cap room to attack it directly.

The mistake now is waiting for a bargain that never arrives. English is the market's control player; Macy and Green control the guard branch; Gilmore and Donaldson split immediate certainty from age value. Once one name goes, the alternatives become more expensive in practical terms even if their contracts do not.
