November Tier 2 Power Rankings: Chelsea Turn The Promotion Race Their Way

Chelsea and Brighton create early promotion separation, while Juventus fall to the bottom despite one loud response against Ajax.

Nina ValeNovember 1982

Tier 2 already has shape. Chelsea and Brighton have separated on record and margin, Marseille sit close enough to make the race uncomfortable, and the bottom has become a weekly test of whether Juventus can turn talent into order.

This board is less about preseason reputation than possession of the promotion line. Chelsea hold it with force. Brighton hold it with efficiency. Everyone else is chasing either proof or repair.

1. Chelsea - 7-1, plus-98

Previous board: up to No. 1. Chelsea's month was the cleanest in the league: seven wins, a W6 finish, and three convincing victories by at least 14. Jack Sikma gives the team a calm interior reference point, but the biggest note is collective control. Even the one-point escape against Juventus counts because promotion teams bank uncomfortable games too.

2. Brighton - 5-2, plus-90

Previous board: up into the automatic line. Brighton have the best average margin in the tier at plus-12.86 and a scorer in Kiki Vandeweghe who makes that offense feel sustainable. They are second because Chelsea have the stronger record, not because Brighton lack a promotion case.

3. Marseille - 4-4, plus-10

Previous board: holding in the chase. Robert Parish gives Marseille a serious spine, and the win over Chelsea is the result that keeps them close to the promotion conversation. The issue is volume: .500 basketball does not move a team above a 5-2 Brighton unless the next month supplies more evidence.

4. FL Fart - 3-4, minus-13

Previous board: slight rise. Larry Drew's side is not above water, but the damage is manageable and the gap to the line is not impossible. FL Fart need the next month to become less about survival and more about pressure, because the current profile reads like a nuisance rather than a promotion threat.

5. Ajax - 3-3, minus-26

Previous board: down from the preseason excitement. Cliff Robinson and Maurice Cheeks give Ajax enough structure to stay mid-board, but the margin is a warning. A team can be level in the standings and still be losing the argument underneath.

6. Manchester United - 3-3, minus-29

Previous board: level but uneasy. Walter Davis and Jeff Ruland are producing the kind of player-level evidence that should travel, yet United's differential says the team has not turned that into clean control. They are safer than the bottom two, not yet close enough to the top two.

7. Monaco - 2-6, minus-110

Previous board: down into trouble. Danny Ainge gives Monaco creation, but the numbers are brutal. A 33-point loss to Chelsea was the cleanest summary of the month: there are nights when Monaco look a full tier away from the promotion standard.

8. Juventus - 1-5, minus-20

Previous board: bottom. Kareem Abdul-Jabbar is still a real reference point and Juventus did beat Ajax by 30. The problem is everything around that win. They lost the rematch to Ajax by 23 and then fell by one at Chelsea, which is precisely how a bad month becomes frustrating rather than simply bad.

Chelsea are not merely leading Tier 2. They have made everyone else explain why the race should still be open.

The next month belongs to the chasers. Brighton can make this a two-team breakaway. Marseille can turn pressure into promotion oxygen. Juventus need to stop being the team with the most interesting excuses.
