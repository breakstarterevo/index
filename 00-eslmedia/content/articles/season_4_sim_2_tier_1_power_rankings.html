December 1984 Tier 1 Power Rankings: Richmond Still Have No Peer

Richmond lost three times in December and somehow strengthened their claim to first; beneath them, the standings and the underlying margins keep disagreeing.

Nina ValeDecember 1984

A power board should become harder to defend as evidence accumulates. AFC Richmond have made the opposite happen. They are 13-4, plus-242 and finished December with three wins, including a 45-point correction of Crystal Palace. The three monthly losses matter. None produced a credible replacement at No. 1.

The order below starts with the full-season profile, uses December to measure direction and then asks which flaws are survivable. That last question is decisive in a tier where five clubs have negative point differentials and the seventh-place team has the third-best margin.

Tier 1 Trust BoardDecember 1984
  1. 1AFC RichmondSTAYED NO. 1
    13-4 overall | 6-3 December | +242 | W3 | Michael Cooper

    Benfica beat them twice and Brighton stole a two-point game. Richmond still won the month by 83, crushed Palace 134-89 and own nearly three times Milan's season differential. Vulnerability has appeared; equality has not.

  2. 2AC MilanSTAYED NO. 2
    10-6 | 5-2 December | +87 | W1 | Bill Laimbeer

    Milan won the first four games of the month, including a 23-point result at Brighton, before Palace and FL Fart exposed the same volatility that haunted November. Second is secure because the response came quickly; first remains distant because Richmond's bad nights are smaller.

  3. 3ChelseaUP 1 FROM NO. 4
    7-11 | 4-5 December | +48 | L2 | Jim Paxson

    This is the board's deliberate argument with the table. Chelsea are in the relegation places, yet their plus-48 differential is better than every team below Milan. A 4-5 month did not fix the record, but it confirmed that the losses have been far less convincing than the standings imply.

  4. 4FL FartUP 3 FROM NO. 7
    8-7 | 4-3 December | -42 | L1 | Larry Drew

    FL Fart followed the November Richmond upset with a winning month and another useful proof, beating Milan 134-130. The negative season margin keeps the rise below Chelsea, but this is no longer a club living off one funny result.

  5. 5BrightonDOWN 2 FROM NO. 3
    8-8 | 3-4 December | -24 | W1 | Kiki Vandeweghe

    Kiki Vandeweghe's 36.6 points per game can rescue almost any evening. It did not rescue a losing month or a season now exactly level. The Christmas win over Richmond is the reason Brighton stop here rather than fall through the middle.

  6. 6Bayern MunichDOWN 1 FROM NO. 5
    7-10 | 2-5 December | -107 | L2 | Bernard King

    Bernard King still supplies thirty points a night. Bayern have turned that gift into seven wins and a minus-107 differential. December's 2-5 record was not unlucky: four defeats came by at least eleven.

  7. 7BenficaUP 1 FROM NO. 8
    6-10 | 4-3 December | -121 | W1 | Moses Malone

    Benfica beat Richmond twice, including the sharpest upset of the month, and went 4-3. The awkward detail is a minus-38 monthly margin: even their recovery arrived with enough heavy losses to keep the season's worst differential intact.

  8. 8Crystal PalaceDOWN 2 FROM NO. 6
    6-9 | 2-5 December | -83 | L1 | Darrell Griffith

    Palace did beat Milan 138-119, the sort of result that should begin a recovery. It sat between a 45-point loss to Richmond and defeats to Brighton and FL Fart. One loud night cannot organize that sequence.

Control Team: AFC Richmond   Biggest Riser: FL Fart   Trust Problem: Chelsea's record   Panic Team: Crystal Palace

January will test whether Chelsea can turn process into safety and whether anybody can turn Richmond's three December losses into an actual transfer of power. Until then, the top of Tier 1 remains the least complicated call in the league.
