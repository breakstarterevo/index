November 1985 Tier 1 Power Rankings: Richmond Take Control

Richmond own the strongest full-season file, Benfica made the largest jump, and the division's preseason assumptions lasted roughly one month.

Nina ValeNovember 1985

The first official month has stripped the word “preseason” from every excuse. AFC Richmond are 7-2 with a plus-72 margin, and the two teams expected to define the board before opening night now sit fifth and seventh. Trust must move with the evidence.

This order begins with the full table, uses Sim 1 margin and opponent quality to separate close records, then asks which weakness is most likely to survive another month. It is not a standings copy: Paris Saint-Germain's positive margin matters, as does AC Milan's ability to beat the leader while losing almost everywhere else.

Tier 1 Trust BoardNovember 1985
  1. 1AFC RichmondUP 1 FROM NO. 2
    7-2 overall | 6-2 Sim 1 | +72 | Michael Cooper

    Richmond won six Sim 1 games and escaped Benfica 104-103, the sort of result that reveals control without pretending it was comfortable. The 125-103 loss at AC Milan is inconvenient evidence, but nobody else pairs seven wins with this margin.

  2. 2BenficaUP 5 FROM NO. 7
    6-3 | 6-3 Sim 1 | +47 | W3 | Moses Malone

    The biggest rise is earned, not cosmetic. Benfica finished on three wins, beat Crystal Palace by 24 and lost only once by more than single digits. Moses Malone supplies the interior certainty; the road record, just 1-2, is the next test of whether this is control or home comfort.

  3. 3Paris Saint-GermainUP 1 FROM NO. 4
    4-4 | 4-3 Sim 1 | +35 | Kelly Tripucka

    PSG have a level record and the tier's third-best point differential. Their 127-95 demolition of Chelsea shows why the board is more interested than the table, while the 14-point Richmond loss shows why interest stops below the top two.

  4. 4Real MadridUP 2 FROM NO. 6
    4-3 | 4-2 Sim 1 | +6 | W2 | Sidney Moncrief

    Real closed with two wins and flattened Brighton 120-95. Sidney Moncrief gives them a two-way answer in tight games. The slim overall margin warns against confusing a clean finish with a dominant month.

  5. 5BrightonDOWN 4 FROM NO. 1
    4-3 | 3-3 Sim 1 | +4 | Kiki Vandeweghe

    Kiki Vandeweghe is scoring 41.1 points on 73.8 percent true shooting, an absurd individual argument. Brighton still finished the sim minus-seven and lost by 25 at Real Madrid. The offense can erase ordinary problems; it did not prevent the best teams from locating the structural ones.

  6. 6ChelseaDOWN 1 FROM NO. 5
    4-6 | 3-6 Sim 1 | -67 | L2 | Jim Paxson

    Chelsea own more wins than the bottom two, but no Tier 1 club produced a worse Sim 1 differential. A 112-100 win over Brighton demonstrates a ceiling. The 44-point loss at Richmond demonstrates how quickly the floor disappears.

  7. 7AC MilanUP 1 FROM NO. 8
    3-7 | 2-6 Sim 1 | -43 | W1 | Joe Barry Carroll

    Joe Barry Carroll is averaging 39.6 points and 4.2 blocks, and Milan handed Richmond their clearest defeat. That combination should produce more than three wins. Until the defense stops turning Carroll's brilliance into expensive decoration, one upset cannot lift Milan above the danger zone.

  8. 8Crystal PalaceDOWN 5 FROM NO. 3
    2-6 | 2-5 Sim 1 | -54 | L2 | Darrell Griffith

    Palace beat Chelsea by 15 and have Alex English creating respectable offense. The rest is a road problem that has become a season problem: six losses in seven away games and the division's worst record.

Control Team: AFC Richmond   Biggest Riser: Benfica   Trust Problem: Brighton   Panic Team: AC Milan

December will ask whether Benfica can travel, whether PSG's margin can become wins and whether Milan can construct an ordinary team around an extraordinary center. Richmond do not need perfection. They need one rival to build a file as complete as theirs.
