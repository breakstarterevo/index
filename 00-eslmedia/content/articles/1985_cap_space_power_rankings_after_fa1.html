After FA1 Cap Space Power Rankings: Inter Milan Can Dictate What Comes Next

Nine contracts changed the roster map, but Inter still owns the largest figure and the clearest route to controlling FA2's elite guard market.

Nina Vale1985 Offseason - After FA1

FA1 distributed more than $926 million in total contract value across nine signings, led by Sidney Moncrief's $143.75 million return to Real Madrid and Kelly Tripucka's $137.5 million agreement with Paris Saint-Germain. Yet the clubs atop the current cap table still have meaningful choices. Inter Milan exits the round with $32.88 million in room, the league's largest listed budget cushion and only two guards on the roster.

AC Milan sits barely behind and may have the larger influence because George Gervin and Larry Bird remain on the open board. The difference is clarity. Inter knows where the shortage is. AC Milan lists needs across guards, wings and the frontcourt, so every large contract removes a different route to completing the roster.

The Post-FA1 Leverage Board
RankClubWhy the room matters
  1. 1Inter Milan

    $32.88M cap room · $162.88M budget room · guard depth needed

    Inter can chase Darrell Griffith, retain World B. Free or pursue a true point guard such as Allen Leavell. Those are distinct roster choices, not cosmetic upgrades. The risk is assuming the deepest position on the board will remain cheap after the first elite deal establishes the scale.

  2. 2AC Milan

    $30.64M cap room · $155.64M budget room · needs at every position group

    No front office faces a more consequential allocation problem. Gervin, Bird and Leavell are all available from last season's group, yet preserving all three would leave less freedom to address the frontcourt. AC Milan can shape FA1, but it cannot behave as if every reunion is independent of the next one.

  3. 3Aston Villa

    $26.97M cap room · $156.97M budget room · frontcourt depth needed

    Villa's perimeter is already deep enough to let the club attack the position of genuine scarcity. Jack Sikma is the obvious premium target, with Bill Cartwright the next established centre on the board. Villa can force that market early instead of joining a guard auction it does not need.

  4. 4Brighton

    $25.47M cap room · $150.47M budget room · frontcourt depth needed

    Seven guards, two wings and two bigs make Brighton's task unusually legible. The club should be measured against the centre and power-forward market, not applauded merely for retaining room. If Villa pushes Sikma's price beyond comfort, Brighton must know whether it prefers Cartwright or Eddie Johnson before the alternatives thin out.

  5. 5Sheffield United

    $21.91M cap room · $151.91M budget room · guard and frontcourt depth needed

    Sheffield can bid on two markets without demanding that one signing solve everything. Retaining Cartwright would stabilise the big rotation; adding Rickey Green or Gerald Henderson would address the other shortage. The concern is that splitting the room may leave the club second in two negotiations.

  6. 6Chelsea

    $14.58M cap room · $139.58M budget room · guard depth needed

    Only one guard is listed on the current roster, which turns a mid-table cap figure into urgent leverage. Chelsea also has Sikma, Green and Norm Nixon in the available class. The club cannot assume familiarity produces a discount; the same scarcity that makes those players valuable to Chelsea makes them useful elsewhere.

  7. 7Real Madrid

    $14.19M cap room · $139.19M budget room · wing depth needed

    Real has five guards, five bigs and one wing. That makes Bird the dream, Greg Ballard the premium alternative and Robert Reid the practical route. The club's leverage comes from knowing exactly which portion of a deep class it can ignore.

  8. 8Monaco

    $11.34M cap room · $161.34M budget room · guard and wing depth needed

    The budget capacity is elite even if the cap room is not. Monaco should benefit when the first wave sends secondary guards and wings looking for cleaner roles. It does not have to win the opening headline to leave FA1 with a more coherent rotation.

  9. 9Sporting CP

    $7.88M cap room · $157.88M budget room · guard depth needed

    Sporting has only two guards and a young core that should make minutes attractive. The smaller cap figure limits access to Griffith's tier, but Leavell, Green or Henderson would each change how the existing scorers are deployed. This is enough room to be decisive, not enough to recover from a slow first choice.

  10. 10Tottenham Hotspur

    $7.70M cap room · $157.70M budget room · wing and frontcourt depth needed

    Tottenham closes the board because the budget is substantial and the needs are specific, but two position groups must be addressed with less than $8 million of cap room. One value deal can help. Paying the opening premium for a single name would leave the roster geometry unchanged.

Control team: Inter Milan. Hardest choice: AC Milan. Cleanest target map: Aston Villa. Timing pressure: Chelsea.
Room Is Not The Same As Freedom

The raw order understates clubs with smaller figures but sharper needs. Chelsea's $14.58 million may be more actionable than a larger total on a balanced roster. Sporting's room carries similar urgency. Conversely, AC Milan's number is enormous until two incumbent stars ask to be paid from it.

Inter begins at No. 1 because it can credibly choose among the elite guard, its own scorer and the point-guard tier. FA1 showed the price of certainty: Mark Aguirre received $86 million from Chelsea, Larry Nance $105.66 million from Aston Villa and Magic Johnson $125 million from Richmond. Unused room still creates options, but FA2 will begin turning those options into deadlines.
