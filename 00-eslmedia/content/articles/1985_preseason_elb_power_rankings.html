1985 ELB Preseason Power Rankings: Ajax Own The Evidence

Ajax combines the tier's best margin with a credible two-way structure, leaving Inter and FL Fart as the first challengers in a division built for movement.

Nina Vale1985 Preseason - ELB

ELB offers two promotion places and one relegation place, which makes the middle of any preseason board unusually important. A club does not need to look dominant to preserve opportunity. It does need a repeatable way to win before the schedule begins separating ambition from structure.

This order gives scoring margin and role clarity more weight than record alone. Ajax leads every useful version of that test. Sheffield, despite employing preseason's most productive player, fails it completely.

1985 ELB Preseason Trust BoardEight Clubs
1AjaxPRESEASON ENTRY
5-1 · +15.5 margin · Cliff Robinson 24.5 PPG, 12.7 RPG · Maurice Cheeks 11.8 APG

Ajax scored 125.3 points per game without reducing the offence to one hot hand. Robinson controlled the interior, Cheeks supplied the passes and Andre Iguodala converted his camp rise into 23.2 points and four assists. The margin is large enough to survive some shooting regression. Ajax's next challenge is less statistical: regular-season opponents will force Iguodala to prove he can punish half-court help after the first action has been taken away.

2Inter MilanPRESEASON ENTRY
4-2 · +5.5 margin · George Gervin 23.8 PPG · Bill Cartwright 11.2 RPG

Inter's camp was not kind to Gervin, but the team did not ask him to score 35 to remain functional. Cartwright stabilised the glass, Norm Nixon created 7.3 assists and the scoring load remained distributed. That is a persuasive preseason shape. Gervin shot 45.8 percent, leaving room for improvement without requiring a new role. Inter ranks second because its ordinary possessions already look competent.

3FL FartPRESEASON ENTRY
5-2 · +3.0 margin · David Thompson 22.9 PPG · Larry Drew 10.0 APG

Seven games gave FL Fart more opportunities to reveal itself, and the answer was reassuringly practical. Drew organised, Jeff Ruland finished and rebounded, and Thompson supplied the perimeter scoring. The concern is a modest margin for a five-win team; several results were controlled without being convincing. That may be enough in a tier where two clubs rise. The first pressure point is whether Ruland's 67.3 percent shooting can fall without taking the half-court offence with it.

4Atletico MadridPRESEASON ENTRY
3-3 · +0.7 margin · Dennis Johnson 28.5 PPG · Johnny Moore 11.5 APG

Atletico's backcourt is the cleanest reason to keep it in the board's upper half. Moore created, Dennis Johnson scored efficiently and James Donaldson gave the group 9.7 rebounds per game. The club was essentially even across six games, which is both evidence of stability and a warning against overrating the guards. Atletico needs a wing to turn competent possessions into advantages before the top three create distance.

5Aston VillaPRESEASON ENTRY
3-3 · -4.8 margin · Larry Nance 29.5 PPG, 10.7 RPG · Trae Young 8.8 APG

Villa owns more high-end production than its margin suggests. Nance was excellent, Xavier McDaniel averaged 28.0 points and 10.0 rebounds, and Young supplied the passing. The minus-4.8 margin says those strengths did not survive every lineup. Villa's frontcourt can overwhelm opponents; the defensive transitions and bench minutes repeatedly returned the advantage. This is the board's clearest candidate to rise once the rotation shortens.

6Bayern MunichPRESEASON ENTRY
2-3 · -2.4 margin · Kevin McHale 27.0 PPG · Tree Rollins 12.6 RPG

Bayern played only five games and left a compact, inconclusive profile. McHale produced, Rollins owned the boards and Kyle Lowry averaged 9.2 assists. The offence still lacked a reliable second scorer when opponents crowded McHale's touches. Bayern is not far from Atletico or Villa, but its path upward requires somebody on the perimeter to become more than a delivery system.

7MarseillePRESEASON ENTRY
2-4 · -3.5 margin · Robert Parish 22.2 PPG, 15.2 RPG · Gus Williams 10.7 APG

Parish and Williams gave Marseille exactly the centre-guard spine a coherent team should want. Jamaal Wilkes added 23.0 points. The club still conceded 122.5 per game, the tier's highest allowance. Those are not mysterious losses caused by a missing creator; they are defensive failures around an otherwise legible offence. Marseille escapes last because the offensive roles already make sense. Its season turns on whether anyone can contain the first drive before Parish has to repair it.

8Sheffield UnitedPRESEASON ENTRY
0-6 · -14.8 margin · Steve Johnson 39.5 PPG, 12.2 RPG · Bimbo Coles 4.2 APG

Johnson delivered the preseason's best individual line and Sheffield turned it into six losses. The passing supply was thin, the defence allowed 118.3 points and every Johnson eruption became another measure of the work left undone. A winless preseason does not sentence a club to relegation. A structure that requires one forward to score 40 merely to keep the game visible might.

Control Team: Ajax. Best Balance: Inter Milan. Likely Riser: Aston Villa. Panic Team: Sheffield United.
The Promotion Shape

Ajax has created the only real preseason separation, but the second promotion place remains available to several different team types. Inter offers balance and few obvious weak minutes. FL Fart offers a functioning guard-centre partnership. Atletico can lean on its backcourt, while Villa has enough frontcourt talent to turn a neutral game into a physical mismatch.

That variety makes margin especially useful. A close record can hide whether a team controlled its wins or merely survived them. FL Fart won five times but finished only plus-three per game. Villa went even while being outscored by 4.8. Atletico was almost exactly neutral. None of those profiles is fatal; each identifies where a promotion case can fracture once opponents stop sharing minutes evenly.

The relegation question is less crowded. Marseille has an identifiable repair—contain the ball before Parish must cover two jobs. Sheffield requires a broader reconstruction. Johnson's production ensures the club has a route to competitive offence, but not yet to competitive basketball. One star can lift a ceiling. ELB's single relegation place will be decided by which club can protect its floor.

Ajax begins with separation, but ELB's two promotion places reward teams that become coherent quickly rather than teams that peak in October. Villa has the talent to jump. Inter has the shape to stay. Sheffield has the clearest instruction of all: build a functioning team around the player who already did his part.
