December 1985 Stock Report: Villa Bought Every Share

Three clubs turned momentum into evidence; three others spent the month discovering that one tolerable night cannot refinance a collapsing season.

Damon CrossDecember 1985

No, this is not complicated. Aston Villa went 8-0. Bayern Munich went 0-8. If a stock report cannot make a judgment there, close the market and turn off the lights.

The interesting work sits beneath the records: which December changed a team's future, which merely decorated it, and which exposed a problem that can no longer be called temporary.

Stock Up
Aston Villa — Promotion Became The Expectation

Eight wins, plus-108, a 13.5-point average margin and ten straight overall. Villa did not sneak through December; they kept turning opponents into supporting evidence. Larry Nance gives them 30 points, 11.5 rebounds and 2.2 blocks, but the decisive change is that his production now lands inside a complete team. Ajax still lead Tier 2 by winning percentage. Villa have made second place feel like the beginning of the argument.

Barcelona — The Chase Is Now Somebody Else's Problem

Barcelona went 6-2 and closed on six straight, including a 33-point beating of Valencia. Bernard King is scoring 30.4 points on 65.7 percent true shooting; Paul Pierce keeps defenses from loading the entire answer onto him. A three-game lead in a one-promotion tier is not permission to relax. It is proof that every challenger now needs Barcelona to participate in its own rescue.

Manchester City — November's Excuse Became December's Run

City entered the month with three official wins and a preseason reputation doing too much of the talking. Six wins later, the case finally belongs to the season. The 131-101 demolition of Juventus showed the ceiling; a three-game closing streak showed continuity. They remain three games behind Barcelona, so this is pressure, not parity. At least it is real pressure now.

Stock Down
Bayern Munich — Stop Calling It A Slump

Zero wins. Eight losses. Minus-153. Twelve straight defeats overall and an 0-11 road record. Kevin McHale can score and Tree Rollins can still defend a possession, but Bayern are not losing because one star needs help around the edges. They are losing by 19.1 points a night because the entire structure has failed. The relegation place is theirs until somebody else proves otherwise.

Chelsea — One Win Cannot Carry Six Games

Chelsea beat Brighton 95-86. Good. They lost the other five, finished minus-106 and absorbed a 132-94 result at Paris Saint-Germain. Jim Paxson's scoring can keep an ordinary game close; December rarely remained ordinary long enough. At 5-11, level with Crystal Palace and six and a half games behind the Tier 1 lead, Chelsea are not unlucky. They are in the bottom two.

Manchester United — The Derby Was A Detour

United beat Manchester City 103-102 in the month's tightest game. Then came six consecutive losses, including a 94-61 disappearance at Monaco. That derby win proves they can still ruin somebody else's evening. A 4-12 record and minus-218 season margin prove they have spent too little time building their own. The next encouraging result has to connect to another one.

Villa changed what promotion requires. Bayern changed how little evidence is needed to identify the relegation favourite.

January will punish any club that treats December as a final verdict. City are still three games back. Chelsea are tied with Palace. Bayern technically have time. But markets are not promises; they are prices. Villa and Barcelona have made belief expensive. Bayern, Chelsea and United have made doubt the easiest purchase in the league.
