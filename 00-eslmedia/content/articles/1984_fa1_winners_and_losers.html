FA1 Winners And Losers: PSG Bought Certainty, The Market Kept Its Teeth

The first wave rewarded clubs that knew exactly what they needed — and increased the pressure on every team still admiring its cap room.

Damon Cross1984 Offseason - After FA1

Stop pretending the first wave is won by the team with the prettiest spreadsheet. It is won by the club that turns money into an answer. Paris Saint-Germain did that twice.

Winner: Paris Saint-Germain

PSG committed $60.2 million over four years to Mike Gminski and $45.675 million over three to Dan Roundfield. That is not subtle. Good. Subtlety does not defend the paint. Roundfield was retained as a proven frontcourt piece, Gminski adds another real center, and the club removed uncertainty before the market could price it higher.

Winner: Chelsea

Jim Paxson received $106 million over five years. That is a major number because Paxson is a major part of Chelsea's structure. He is 24, carries a 137 overall profile, and now stays beside Jack Sikma and Norm Nixon. Chelsea still needs depth, but it did not allow a foundational guard to become somebody else's solution.

Winner, With A Receipt: AC Milan

Joe Barry Carroll at $138.75 million over five years and Bill Laimbeer at $90.3 million over four is a frontcourt declaration. Milan already has Larry Bird and George Gervin. Now it has committed heavily to size and continuity. The basketball logic is obvious; so is the cost. Milan wins FA1 because the roster got harder to move. The receipt matters because its listed cap position is now $4.31 million over the line and the remaining need is on the perimeter.

Winner: Brighton

Kiki Vandeweghe is not the kind of scorer a contender casually replaces. Brighton's five-year, $143.75 million commitment keeps its offensive centerpiece out of the market. Expensive? Of course. Losing him would have been more expensive in basketball terms.

Loser: Passive Cap Space

Atletico Madrid has $33.65 million in room. Aston Villa has $31.97 million. Inter Milan has $28.62 million and the largest overall budget cushion. Those numbers are not trophies. They are deadlines.

English, Short, Ballard, Macy, Gilmore and Donaldson remain available. That means the cap-rich clubs are not losers for missing a closed window. They are losers only if patience turns into paralysis. But the pressure has changed: every rival now knows who has money, who has holes and who cannot leave the next phase empty-handed.

Loser: The Bargain Fantasy

The board still has stars, which sounds like good news until six teams chase the same two wings and the same two guards. Prices do not fall because a front office waits politely. They fall when alternatives exist. At wing, English and Ballard own the leverage. At point guard, Macy and Rickey Green can make bidders nervous. At center, Gilmore offers immediate certainty while Donaldson offers youth.

The first wave was not the whole offseason. It was the warning shot. PSG, Chelsea, Milan and Brighton paid to protect certainty. Now the teams with room must prove they kept it for a reason.
