FA2 Winners And Losers: Inter Bought The Middle, Villa Kept Waiting

Inter and Palace found useful answers without surrendering flexibility, while the league's richest shopper discovered that unused room can become its own risk.

Damon Cross1984 Offseason - After FA2

Stop pretending every free-agent win needs fireworks. Inter Milan came through FA2 understanding the assignment: buy actual basketball players, fix the middle of the roster and keep tomorrow's panic out of today's contract.

Purvis Short, Greg Ballard, Kyle Macy, Artis Gilmore and Ray Williams all arrived or returned on one-year, $5 million deals. Read that list again. A prime forward with 138 potential, a 131-overall wing, two capable guards and a veteran center, all without a multi-year trap. That is not shopping. That is clearing the shelf while everybody else argues over the label.

Winner: Inter Milan

The case is simple. Inter entered the wave needing optional upgrades and left with useful depth across the rotation. World B. Free remains the star. Short and Ballard now give him real forward support. Macy and Williams protect the guard minutes. Gilmore supplies size. No single signing has to save the club, which is precisely why the group works.

Winner: Crystal Palace

Palace kept Alex English for $30.75 million over two years, added Rolando Blackman for $3 million, found Julius Erving for $1.35 million and retained Eddie Johnson for one year. English was the market's best available scorer. Palace did not let its own star become somebody else's solution.

Was $5 million for Eddie Johnson aggressive? Yes. Does that erase the value of English, Blackman and Erving? Absolutely not. Palace turned wing uncertainty into surplus and did it without tying every move to a long contract.

Winner: Brighton's Depth Plan

Brighton did not chase one saving move. It added Jawann Oldham, Greg Anthony, Mike Harper, Lorenzo Romar, Oliver Miller and Bobby Jones on short deals, then kept building around Kiki Vandeweghe. That is six distinct rotation bets without one contract dictating the next four years.

Not every name will matter by spring. That is the point. Brighton bought options, not obligations, and FA2 is exactly where that approach should be rewarded.

Winner, With A Warning: Real Madrid

Kareem Abdul-Jabbar at $2 million for one year is the kind of veteran bet contenders should make. Wayne Cooper at $6.3 million over three years is the sturdier investment, while Jay Vincent, Rex Walters, Elvin Hayes and Clemon Johnson deepen the floor. Madrid shopped with a plan.

The warning is positional. The roster still shows only one wing. A pile of sensible additions does not make that fact disappear. Finish the job.

The Expensive Middle: AC Milan And PSG

AC Milan committed $138.75 million over five years to Joe Barry Carroll and $90.3 million over four to Bill Laimbeer. PSG spent $60.2 million over four on Mike Gminski and $45.675 million over three on Dan Roundfield. These are serious players and serious solutions. They are also serious contracts.

This is not a cheap declaration that spending is bad. Certainty costs money. But long deals must solve tomorrow as well as today, and both clubs now carry less room for error if age, fit or development bends the wrong way. They did not lose FA2. They accepted the most pressure from it.

Loser: Aston Villa's Clock

Aston Villa still has $31.97 million in cap room, the most in the league. It also has two guards, one big and a remaining market led by Rickey Green instead of Alex English. That matters.

Villa has not lost the offseason. It can outbid the room, split its money across Green and a frontcourt piece, or hunt value as camps approach. But the first wave removed Short, Ballard, Macy, Gilmore, English and other clean answers. Every option left now carries more compromise. Money is leverage only when the player you need is still available.

Verdict

Inter won the wave by buying the middle. Palace won by keeping the top. Madrid won by finding veteran value. Milan and PSG bought certainty at prices that will be judged for years. Villa kept the biggest wallet and watched the best aisle empty.

The next wave is where that last verdict can change. If Villa lands Green and useful size, patience becomes discipline. If it waits again, do not call the room flexibility. Call it what it is: unused.
