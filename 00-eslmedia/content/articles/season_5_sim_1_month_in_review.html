Month In Review - Season 5 - Sim 1

November 1985 Review: Barcelona's 52-Point Warning

The season's first official month produced one enormous score, three credible leaders and enough awkward evidence to keep every division unsettled.

Graham TrentNovember 1985

Manchester United arrived in Barcelona on 11 November and left with the season reduced to a single, rather unhelpful number: 52. Barcelona won 134-82, the largest margin of the sim, and spent the rest of November making the result look less like an accident than an announcement.

They finished the month on four straight wins and own a plus-105 full-season differential. Their one genuinely awkward result, a 101-85 defeat at Tottenham Hotspur, is useful precisely because it prevents the early promotion race from becoming ceremonial.

Richmond Learned To Win Small And Large

AFC Richmond's most revealing victories sat at opposite ends of the scale. They escaped Benfica 104-103 in the closest game of November, then beat Chelsea 126-82 in their largest win. One required poise; the other required none after halftime.

The 7-2 record and plus-72 margin make Richmond Tier 1's first leader. AC Milan's 125-103 win over them complicates the portrait. It does not overturn it. Richmond were the only Tier 1 club to combine the best record with the best margin, and Michael Cooper's presence gives an already balanced roster a dependable defensive shape.

Tier 2 Refused To Choose One Story

FL Fart posted the strongest Sim 1 record at 7-3. Inter Milan went 5-1 with a plus-78 margin. Ajax finished 5-2 and won its last three. The table gives Inter and Ajax the two promotion places; the month gives FL Fart a perfectly reasonable objection.

Larry Drew's 25 points and 10.9 assists have made FL Fart's pace coherent. Andre Iguodala is scoring 29.8 points with remarkable efficiency for Ajax. Inter's best quality may be less glamorous: four road games, four road wins.

Atletico Madrid supplied the division's strangest footnote. Their only victory was the sharpest upset of the month, 123-112 at Inter. They lost the other five Sim 1 games. A result that should have launched a recovery instead became proof that their best level exists and rarely visits.

The Individual Numbers Arrived Early

Kiki Vandeweghe averaged 41.1 points for Brighton on 73.8 percent true shooting. Joe Barry Carroll answered with 39.6 points and 4.2 blocks for AC Milan. Steve Johnson gave AC Sparta Praha 35.1 points and 10.9 rebounds. None of their teams ended November in a promotion place or atop Tier 1.

Moses Malone's 33.9 points and 11.4 rebounds came with a 6-3 Benfica record, which makes his line less spectacular only until the standings are included. November's first awards argument is not about whether the league has enough stars. It is about how much losing their numbers can survive.

The Bottoms Already Feel Different

Crystal Palace and AC Milan occupy Tier 1's two relegation places. Atletico hold Tier 2's single drop spot. Manchester United sit last in Tier 3, where relegation is not the threat but irrelevance in a one-promotion race may be nearly as punishing.

United were outscored by 94 across six Sim 1 games. Bayern Munich lost four straight to finish the month. Chelsea suffered the worst Sim 1 differential in Tier 1 despite owning more wins than Milan or Palace. These are not verdicts after one month. They are expensive problems that December cannot ignore.

The loudest result belonged to Barcelona. The most complete month belonged to nobody, which is why every race still has room to become interesting.

November gave the league leaders, lines and a first set of uncomfortable questions. Richmond must show that Milan's upset was local. Barcelona must keep Valencia at arm's length. Tier 2 must decide which of three excellent openings gets only two rewards. The season has begun by refusing simplicity.
