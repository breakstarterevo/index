November 1985 Race Watch: The Lines Are Already Moving

The first official table has produced clear occupants at every line, but games played and tiny gaps make none of those positions comfortable.

Nina ValeNovember 1985

The rules are fixed even when the early table is not: Tier 1 relegates two clubs, Tier 2 promotes two and relegates one, and Tier 3 promotes one. November's standings therefore matter more than any isolated hot streak. Form explains how a club reached the line; the full table decides whether it is actually there.

The uneven number of games is the first complication. FL Fart have seven wins and sit behind six-win Ajax on winning percentage. AC Milan and Crystal Palace are level in games behind despite playing different totals. This is a pressure map, not a forecast carved into stone.

Tier 1: Milan And Palace Are Down Today

AC Milan are seventh at 3-7. Crystal Palace are eighth at 2-6. Those are the two relegation places, both 4.5 games behind AFC Richmond. Chelsea sit immediately above them at 4-6, one win clear of Milan after the same number of games; their minus-6.7 average scoring margin keeps the danger real.

Brighton are fourth at 4-3 and only one game closer to the leader than Chelsea, but the games-in-hand difference is substantial. Their level scoring margin makes them a nervous observer, not a line team. Milan's win over Richmond is useful evidence of a ceiling; it does not move a 3-7 club to safety.

Tier 2 Promotion: Three Good Teams, Two Places

Inter Milan lead at 6-1, with Ajax second at 6-2. Those are the promotion positions. FL Fart sit third at 7-4, one game behind Inter in the official table despite owning the division's highest win total. Their 7-3 Sim 1 run is genuine momentum, but it cannot be used to promote them past Ajax while the standings say otherwise.

1. Inter Milan
6-1 | promotion leader | +11.6 average margin
2. Ajax
6-2 | second promotion place | +8.8
3. FL Fart
7-4 | 1.0 GB | +6.5

Sheffield United, Aston Villa and Marseille are all three games behind the leader. That is a chase, not immediate line pressure. The top three have separated because each owns at least six wins and a positive margin; everyone below them owns a losing record.

Tier 2 Relegation: Atletico Have One Win

Atletico Madrid occupy the only relegation place at 1-6. Bayern Munich are seventh at 2-6, half a game ahead. Sheffield and Villa, both 4-5, sit three games behind the leader but hold a far more useful cushion over Atletico.

The awkward counterpoint is Atletico's 123-112 win at Inter, the sharpest upset of November. It proves they can disrupt the tier's best team. Five other Sim 1 losses and a minus-54 full-season differential prove that the disruption has not travelled from one night to the table.

Tier 3 Promotion: Barcelona Have Company

Barcelona hold the division's single promotion place at 6-2. Valencia are one game back at 5-3, Juventus 1.5 back at 4-3, and Tottenham two back at 4-4. Those four are live. Monaco and Sporting CP, both 2.5 games behind, need a strong December before proximity becomes a serious claim.

Tottenham's 101-85 win over Barcelona matters because it gives the chase a direct route to belief. Barcelona's plus-105 point differential matters more because it describes eight games rather than one. Valencia remain the nearest threat; Juventus must end a three-game slide before their game in hand becomes leverage.

November created occupants, not owners: every race line is real, and every gap is still small enough to punish one careless sim.

December's most consequential games will be the ordinary ones. Milan cannot live on beating Richmond if Chelsea keep collecting the wins around them. FL Fart cannot celebrate the best month while Inter and Ajax hold the places. Barcelona cannot treat a one-game lead like distance.
