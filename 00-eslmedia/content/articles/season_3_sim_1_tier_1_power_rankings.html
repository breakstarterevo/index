November 1983 Tier 1 Power Rankings: Richmond Take The Room

AFC Richmond own the first full-season trust board at 7-0, while Benfica chase and Bayern fall into early danger.

Nina ValeSeason 3 - Sim 1

Tier 1 finally has results sturdy enough to judge. AFC Richmond move to the top because 7-0 with a plus-140 differential is not decoration. It is the cleanest evidence anyone has put on the floor.

This is not a prediction board. It is a trust board: what the standings say, what the margins support, and what the next sim can expose. Richmond have won big without needing excuses. Benfica stay close because their file still looks contender-grade. Bayern fall because talent does not count as safety when the relegation line is already visible.

Tier 1 Board - November 1983
  1. 1AFC Richmond
    UP 1 FROM NO. 2 | 7-0 | +140 | W7 | Michael Cooper
    Richmond have the rare opening file with no messy footnote: unbeaten, four double-digit wins, and a 40-point hit on Sheffield United. Michael Cooper gives the roster a two-way spine, but the larger point is control. The first real test now is psychological: can they play with every opponent aiming at their number?
  2. 2Benfica
    DOWN 1 FROM NO. 1 | 6-2 | +60 | W3 | Moses Malone
    Benfica are still sturdy enough to argue with the top spot, especially with Moses Malone anchoring the middle and a three-game winning streak quieting the early wobble. The Sheffield loss is why they slide behind Richmond. A contender can lose; a No. 1 team should not be giving the bottom half that clean a headline.
  3. 3AC Milan
    STAYED NO. 3 | 5-4 | +36 | W1 | Larry Bird
    Milan are not as clean as the top two, but plus-36 matters when four of the nine games have been tight. Larry Bird gives the attack shape, Joe Barry Carroll gives it violence at the rim, and the 145-111 road win over Sheffield showed the ceiling. The concern is inconsistency, not quality.
  4. 4Real Madrid
    UP 2 FROM NO. 6 | 4-3 | -45 | W2 | Sidney Moncrief
    Real Madrid are the uncomfortable riser: winning record, ugly differential. Sidney Moncrief keeps them organized enough to sit above the danger group, but the minus-45 says the losses have been too loud. They are fourth because the room below them did less, not because the file is finished.
  5. 5Chelsea
    UP 2 FROM NO. 7 | 3-3 | +8 | W1 | Jack Sikma
    Chelsea's file is almost suspiciously balanced: .500 record, positive differential, one clean win over Sheffield and two Richmond losses that do not need over-reading. Jack Sikma makes them difficult to dismiss. The next step is beating someone above the survival line.
  6. 6FL Fart
    DOWN 1 FROM NO. 5 | 2-6 | -47 | L4 | Larry Drew
    FL Fart have one result that still matters, the 127-97 win over Real Madrid. Everything around it has started to fray. The four-game slide is the problem, and the Richmond losses made the case feel wider than one place on a table.
  7. 7Sheffield United
    STAYED NO. 8 | 2-6 | -124 | L3 | Rickey Green
    Sheffield climb only because Bayern made the bottom complicated. Rickey Green can still make possessions look professional, and the Benfica win is real, but a minus-124 differential is not a typo you can charm away. The defense has turned too many nights into damage control.
  8. 8Bayern Munich
    DOWN 4 FROM NO. 4 | 1-6 | -28 | L3 | Bernard King
    Bayern are the strange panic team because the margin is not as bad as the record. Bernard King keeps the ceiling alive, and the road win at Milan is not nothing. But 1-6 is 1-6, and Tier 1 does not give survival credit for looking dangerous while losing.
Board Notes
Control Team: AFC Richmond | Biggest Riser: Real Madrid | Trust Problem: FL Fart | Panic Team: Bayern Munich

Richmond are the only team with authority. Benfica and Milan are the only teams close enough to challenge it right now. The bottom four are already sorting themselves by who can turn a decent argument into actual wins.

Richmond have the cleanest file in Tier 1. Bayern have the strangest one.

December is where Richmond's unbeaten start either becomes status or starts collecting caveats. For everyone else, the question is simpler: are you chasing the top, or are you quietly counting the relegation line?
