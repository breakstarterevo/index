November Tier 1 Power Rankings: Madrid Own The First Real Board

Real Madrid separate from the Champions League field, while Richmond hold second and the relegation line already has real heat.

Nina ValeNovember 1982

The first proper Tier 1 board has one clean rule: reward the results, then ask whether the profile can survive the next month. Real Madrid pass both parts more convincingly than anyone else.

Madrid went 6-1 with a plus-108 margin, beat AC Milan twice by a combined 57 points, and closed with three straight wins. Sidney Moncrief gives the board a star anchor, but the case is broader than one scorer. This is the only Tier 1 team that currently combines record, margin and recent form without needing a large caveat.

1. Real Madrid - 6-1, plus-108

Previous board: up from the chasing pack to No. 1. The loss at Atletico Madrid keeps this from being a coronation, but everything around it is loud: 130-98 over AC Milan, 123-115 over AFC Richmond, and a 25-point answer against Atletico. Moncrief's two-way stability makes Madrid feel repeatable rather than merely hot.

2. AFC Richmond - 5-3, minus-18

Previous board: down one spot. Richmond do not have Madrid's margin profile, but Magic Johnson still gives them the best organizer in the tier and enough high-end quality to stay second. The concern is simple: a 5-3 record with negative differential usually means the wins are carrying more stress than the table admits.

3. Sheffield United - 4-3, plus-33

Previous board: up into the top three. Sheffield United are not as glamorous as the names below them, but Rickey Green's group has the best middle-class case in the tier. A winning record and positive margin matter when Milan, Benfica and Bayern are all still trying to make their profiles line up.

4. Benfica - 4-4, plus-11

Previous board: level. Moses Malone is doing MVP-level work and that alone keeps Benfica above the mess. The record is ordinary, yet the margin is not damaging and the roster has a clear identity. If the supporting results catch up to Malone's production, Benfica can climb quickly.

5. AC Milan - 4-4, minus-4

Previous board: down from the contender shelf. Larry Bird gives Milan the highest ceiling argument in the room, and the 143-95 demolition of Crystal Palace showed what the best version looks like. The problem is that Madrid also beat them 108-83 and 130-98. That is not a footnote. That is the current gap.

6. Bayern Munich - 3-3, plus-14

Previous board: slight drop. Bernard King and Kevin McHale make Bayern too talented to panic over a level start, and the positive differential suggests there is more here than the record. Still, a team this sturdy on paper needs to turn respectable losses into table pressure before it can move higher.

7. Atletico Madrid - 2-5, minus-50

Previous board: down into danger. Atletico own the best single upset of the month after beating Real Madrid 107-103, but one sharp result cannot erase the rest of the evidence. James Donaldson's club is on the relegation line with a poor margin and a two-game losing streak.

8. Crystal Palace - 1-6, minus-94

Previous board: bottom. Darrell Griffith can score, but Palace's month was defined by separation. The 48-point loss at AC Milan was the headline, and the L4 finish makes it feel less like one bad night than a pattern.

Madrid have earned the top spot because their best results came against the teams trying to share the room.

The next board will test whether Richmond can clean up the margin, whether Milan can stop leaking credibility in elite matchups, and whether Atletico can turn one upset into a rescue plan. For now, Madrid own the board.
