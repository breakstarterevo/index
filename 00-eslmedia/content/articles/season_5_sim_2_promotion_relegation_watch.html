December 1985 Race Watch: Villa Changed The Price Of Promotion

The occupants are clear, the pressure groups are smaller than the table suggests, and one perfect month has made Tier 2's two places brutally expensive.

Nina ValeDecember 1985

The rules come first. Tier 1 relegates two teams. Tier 2 promotes two and relegates one. Tier 3 promotes one. The standings—not December form, not point differential and not the mood after a large win—define every current line.

Form explains pressure. It does not move the line by itself. With that distinction in place, the league has one crowded relegation fight, one increasingly exclusive promotion race and two lines where the occupant has begun to separate.

Tier 1 Relegation: Palace And Chelsea Hold The Bottom Two
Current line: Crystal Palace 5-11 and Chelsea 5-11
AC Milan 6-11, 0.5 games clear | Brighton 7-8, 1.5 clear

Palace and Chelsea are tied at the bottom, with Milan only half a game ahead. That is the real pressure group. Brighton are close enough to sweat but have a positive scoring margin and one fewer game played; their profile is materially healthier than the three clubs below them.

December complicated Palace in the useful way. They went 3-5, lost the month by only 21 and beat AFC Richmond 119-118. The upset proves their ceiling. Sixteen games prove the floor. Chelsea went 1-5 and minus-106, leaving no similar ambiguity. Milan's 3-4 month kept them above the line by the smallest possible amount, but a minus-126 season margin gives them no reason to feel protected.

What changes next: one Chelsea or Palace run would immediately pull Milan into the bottom two. Brighton have room, not immunity.

Tier 2 Promotion: Ajax And Villa Control It
Current places: Ajax 11-3 and Aston Villa 12-5
Inter Milan 10-6, 2.0 games back | FL Fart 11-7, 2.0 back

Ajax own first by winning percentage. Villa own the hottest form in the league after an 8-0 month and ten consecutive wins overall. Their plus-181 and plus-93 season differentials respectively give both places structural support rather than merely a friendly week on the schedule.

Inter and FL Fart are level at two games back, close enough to be live and far enough to need help. Inter's balanced 5-3 road record travels better than FL Fart's 2-6 mark. FL Fart's 11 wins and plus-111 margin prevent that split from becoming a dismissal. These are credible chasers; they are no longer co-favourites.

What changes next: the direct meetings matter, but the first requirement is stopping the leaders' streaks. Villa and Ajax have won fifteen straight games between them.

Tier 2 Relegation: Bayern Are Alone Until Proven Otherwise
Current line: Bayern Munich 2-14
Atletico Madrid 4-10, 3.0 games clear | AC Sparta Praha 7-10, 3.5 clear

Bayern are last, have lost twelve straight and remain winless in eleven road games. December's 0-8 record did not create the line; it stretched the distance to safety. Atletico are three games clear despite sitting seventh, a gap large enough to make them a pressure team rather than a co-occupant.

AC Sparta Praha's poor month and minus-140 season margin are concerning, but the standings place them three and a half games clear. Calling this a three-team race would confuse aesthetic discomfort with actual danger.

What changes next: Bayern need a winning run before the teams above them need to panic. One isolated victory will end the streak, not the separation.

Tier 3 Promotion: Barcelona Have Built A Cushion
Current place: Barcelona 12-4
Arsenal 8-6 and Manchester City 9-7, both 3.0 games back | Valencia 9-8, 3.5 back

Barcelona hold the only promotion place and have won six straight. Arsenal and Manchester City are tied three games back despite different records because of games played. Both went sharply upward in December: Arsenal 5-2, City 6-2. They are the closest credible challengers.

Valencia sit another half-game back, but a 4-5 month and two-game losing streak point away from the line. Monaco are four back and need more than a three-game streak to join the central chase. The table is close enough to remain alive and separated enough to identify who controls it.

What changes next: Barcelona must start losing. The challengers cannot erase three games through good form alone while the leader keeps winning.

A race is not every team that can see the line. It is every team close enough to change it before the leader changes the subject.

January's cleanest pressure belongs to Inter and FL Fart, because two promotion places remain within two games. The harshest belongs to Bayern, because three games of safety are paired with the league's longest losing streak. Barcelona own the greatest control. Palace, Chelsea and Milan own the least room. The lines are no longer provisional; they are beginning to demand responses.
