December 1985 Review: Aston Villa Refused To Lose

Eight Villa wins turned Tier 2 upside down, while Barcelona tightened its grip and Bayern discovered how quickly a bad month can become a season.

Graham TrentDecember 1985

Aston Villa began December in fourth place and ended it having misplaced the concept of defeat. Eight games brought eight wins, a plus-108 margin and the sort of quiet administrative cruelty that makes every opponent feel like an appointment rather than a contest.

The first win came by one at Atletico Madrid. The next seven removed the ambiguity. Villa beat Bayern Munich twice, Marseille twice and AC Sparta Praha twice, winning those six by a combined 100 points. Larry Nance supplied the dependable centre of it all—30 points, 11.5 rebounds and 2.2 blocks for the season—but the streak was larger than one star catching fire. Villa defended, travelled and kept finding a second answer after Nance had provided the first.

Tier 2 Acquired A Second Favourite

Ajax still own first place by winning percentage at 11-3, and their plus-181 differential remains the tier's cleanest full-season evidence. Andre Iguodala is scoring 27.6 points on 66.2 percent true shooting, Maurice Cheeks is organizing everything around him, and five straight wins have erased the one December loss to FL Fart.

Villa, however, are now 12-5 and have won ten straight overall. That is not a hot team approaching the promotion race; it is one of the two teams controlling it. Inter Milan and FL Fart sit two games back. Both remain credible. Neither can pretend December did not raise the price of entry.

At the other end, Bayern Munich turned a difficult opening month into an emergency. They went 0-8, were outscored by 153 and finished with a 128-90 loss at Ajax as the clearest image of the problem. Their road record is 0-11. Kevin McHale and Tree Rollins can still produce respectable individual nights, but respectability has become detached from winning. Twelve consecutive losses have made the single Tier 2 relegation place feel less like a line than an address.

Richmond Lead, But The Division Can Reach Them

AFC Richmond remain the Tier 1 standard at 12-4. They went 5-2 in December, finished plus-69 and answered AC Milan's November upset with a 99-78 correction. Nothing in that profile suggests a leader losing control.

December did supply two useful objections. Crystal Palace won 119-118 in Richmond, the month's sharpest upset, and Paris Saint-Germain beat them 131-117 later in the schedule. PSG finished 5-2 and now sit 9-6 with the division's second-best scoring margin. Benfica went 5-3 and remain only a game and a half back. Richmond still own the room; the room has stopped being empty.

The bottom is more severe. Chelsea managed one win in six games and lost the month by 106 points. AC Milan and Crystal Palace each won three times, enough to keep the two relegation places crowded. Palace's Richmond upset is the inconvenient detail: a team can be dangerous on Tuesday and still be 5-11 by Friday. The table records the latter.

Barcelona Made Control Look Routine

Barcelona's November included the league's loudest result. December was more useful. Six wins in eight games, a plus-93 margin and a six-game closing streak moved them to 12-4, three games clear of the nearest Tier 3 challengers. Bernard King is averaging 30.4 points with startling efficiency; Paul Pierce gives the attack another scorer who does not need the same possession twice.

Arsenal and Manchester City both improved the argument beneath them. Arsenal went 5-2 behind Chris Bosh and now own a positive margin on both sides of the month. City went 6-2, won three straight and transformed November's disappointment into a promotion chase that at least deserves to be spoken aloud.

Yet the best evidence still belongs to Barcelona. Their 131-98 win over Valencia was a direct demolition of a contender. Tottenham Hotspur did beat them by 12, proving the leader can be compressed. Barcelona responded by not losing again.

December's defining team was Villa. Its defining fact was broader: every race now has a club forcing everyone else to play faster.
The Month's Strange Edges

Manchester United produced the closest finish, a 103-102 win at Manchester City, then lost six straight to close the month. Valencia produced the biggest win, 136-94 over Juventus, and still finished December below .500. Those results are reminders that a schedule can hold a perfect night without offering a coherent month.

January begins with Richmond, Ajax and Barcelona in first. Only Barcelona have meaningful room. Richmond must hold off Benfica and PSG; Ajax must account for a Villa side that has forgotten how to stop; and Bayern must find one road win before the standings make hope sound sentimental. December did not settle the league. It clarified who is allowed to set the terms.
