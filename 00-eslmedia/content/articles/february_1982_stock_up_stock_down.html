European Super League - Stock Report
February 1982

Stock Up, Stock Down: Benfica Bought Control

February's risers looked like teams taking charge of their tier, while the fallers finally lost the benefit of the doubt that had been protecting them for weeks.

Graham TrentFebruary 1982 stock board

The only useful way to write a stock column is verdict first. Who changed the room they walk into now? Benfica did. Inter Milan did. FL Fart did. Aston Villa, Arsenal, and Marseille also changed the room, just in the wrong direction.

Stock Up: Benfica

7-1 February | +13.75 average margin

Benfica did not just win the month. They owned it. The 147-110 demolition of Marseille made Moses Malone's MVP case louder and turned the promotion race from crowded to threatening.

Stock Down: Aston Villa

0-6 February | Six-game slide

Villa are not merely losing; they are losing in ways that keep removing oxygen from the room. When even the close call with Benfica still ends as another loss, the relegation file starts to look structural.

Stock Up: Inter Milan

5-2 February | +62 point differential

Inter took the strongest version of the Tier 3 argument. They beat Barcelona by 25, crushed Sporting by 35, and made promotion look like a standard rather than a hot streak. World B. Free keeps the whole thing pointed forward.

Stock Down: Arsenal

1-6 February | -15.57 average margin

One late win over Inter does not fix the month. Sporting spent too much of February getting blown off the floor, and that turns every future positive result into a request for a second opinion.

Stock Up: FL Fart

5-2 February | 19-9 overall

The record still matters. FL Fart stayed level with the best of the tier, kept a strong scoring margin, and remain fully live in the promotion race. They are not the loudest team in Tier 3, but they may be the steadiest pressure source.

Stock Down: Marseille

1-6 February | -98 point differential

This is the harshest sell because it came from a team with first-place credibility. Marseille lost big to Chelsea, Benfica, Juventus, and Ajax, and by the end of the month they looked less like a promotion lock than a warning about how fast a table can turn.

The Verdict

The best buy is Benfica because the results came with authority. The ugliest sell is Marseille because the collapse damaged both their record and the trust around it.
