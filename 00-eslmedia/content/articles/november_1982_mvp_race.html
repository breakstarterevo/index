November MVP Race: Moses Malone Opens With The Hardest Case

Moses Malone leads the first MVP board, with Robert Parish and Joe Barry Carroll setting the pace in the lower tiers.

Damon CrossNovember 1982

The first MVP ladder is not about vibes. It is about production, availability, team context, and whether the numbers actually move the room. Through November, Moses Malone has the strongest case in the league.

There are three races because the tiers create three different jobs. Tier 1 asks who can carry against the league's hardest competition. Tier 2 asks who can turn numbers into promotion pressure. Tier 3 asks who can own a one-seat climb.

Tier 1 ballot

1. Moses Malone, Benfica. This is the cleanest individual case on the board: 35.0 points, 13.2 rebounds, 3.3 blocks and a true shooting mark over .610. Benfica are only 4-4, and that is the hesitation, but Malone is the reason they are not ordinary.

2. Magic Johnson, AFC Richmond. Magic is averaging 24.9 points, 7.7 rebounds, 9.6 assists and 2.5 steals on outrageous efficiency. Richmond's negative margin keeps him behind Malone, but the all-court argument is real.

3. Adrian Dantley, Atletico Madrid. Dantley has 30.7 points a game on strong shooting, and Atletico's upset of Real Madrid gives him one signature team moment. The caveat is heavy: Atletico are 2-5, and an MVP case cannot live forever below the line.

4. Bernard King, Bayern Munich. King is at 27.2 points and 7.6 rebounds, giving Bayern a steady scoring claim. The team is only 3-3, which leaves him in the chase group rather than the lead.

5. Sidney Moncrief, Real Madrid. Moncrief's raw line, 25.4 points, 5.7 rebounds, 3.6 assists and 2.0 steals, belongs higher if team context dominates. Madrid are 6-1 and plus-108. The only reason he is fifth is that the top four have louder individual volume.

Tier 2 ballot

1. Robert Parish, Marseille. Parish is giving Marseille 23.0 points, 14.9 rebounds, 3.5 assists and 2.6 blocks. That is a full big-man portfolio, and it keeps a .500 team connected to the promotion race.

2. Jeff Ruland, Manchester United. Ruland's 25.5 points and 11.5 rebounds are too large to ignore, especially with efficient finishing. United's middling team profile keeps him from first.

3. Kiki Vandeweghe, Brighton. Vandeweghe has the clean scoring case: 28.7 points on elite efficiency for a 5-2 Brighton side with a plus-90 margin. If Brighton stay on the promotion line, he can jump this ballot quickly.

4. Maurice Cheeks, Ajax. Cheeks is not here because of points. He is here because 16.5 points, 7.3 assists and 3.5 steals gives Ajax direction on both ends. The minus-26 team margin is the brake.

5. Jack Sikma, Chelsea. Sikma's 22.1 points, 10.1 rebounds and 1.6 blocks sit inside the best team month in Tier 2. Chelsea's balance suppresses the individual spotlight, but it also strengthens his winning case.

Tier 3 ballot

1. Joe Barry Carroll, Paris Saint-Germain. Carroll leads Tier 3 because 28.7 points, 8.7 rebounds and efficient scoring have PSG in the live promotion group. That is production with consequence.

2. Calvin Natt, Manchester City. Natt's 30.8 points and 8.4 rebounds are the biggest scoring case in the tier. The problem is City at 2-6. He is carrying, but the ladder still has to ask where.

3. Buck Williams, Arsenal. Williams is averaging 24.9 points and 11.0 rebounds on .617 shooting for a 5-2 Sporting side. That is the best blend of winning and efficiency in the tier, and it could be No. 1 by December.

4. Jalen Duren, Barcelona. Duren's 17.6 points and 12.8 rebounds come with excellent efficiency. Barcelona's losing record keeps this from becoming a top-three case right now.

5. World B. Free, Inter Milan. Free is at 29.7 points and Inter are close enough to the promotion race for that to matter. The shot profile is less clean than the names above him, but the pressure he creates is real.

The strongest statistical case is Malone. The strongest team-context riser is Moncrief. The most dangerous next jump might be Kiki Vandeweghe or Buck Williams.

November gives the ladder a starting point, not a verdict. December will tell us which candidates can turn numbers into leverage, and which ones are just producing inside somebody else's story.
