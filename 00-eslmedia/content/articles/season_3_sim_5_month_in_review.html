March 1984 Review: Milan Keep Charging And The Lines Harden

Richmond still own the league, Milan made the chase louder, and March pushed the bottom of every tier toward uncomfortable honesty.

Graham TrentSeason 3 - Sim 5

March did not overthrow the European Super League. It did something more precise: it removed several polite excuses. AFC Richmond went 8-1 and remain miles clear at 33-4. AC Milan went 6-0 and made the word "chase" feel less ornamental. Paris Saint-Germain still lead the Conference League. Brighton and Crystal Palace still look like the Europa League's two promotion-grade teams.

The best team in the table is still Richmond, whose 33-4 record and plus-11.6 differential have turned Tier 1 into a long argument over second place. The month included a one-point escape at Real Madrid, a 33-point hammering of Sheffield United, and a three-point win over Benfica. That is a useful range: close-game nerve, bottom-table punishment, and contender control.

Milan Make The Chase Respectable

AC Milan's 6-0 month was the cleanest run in the top tier by record, margin, and mood. The streak is now W10, the season record is 24-12, and the monthly margin was plus-83. George Gervin and Joe Barry Carroll have turned Milan from a second-place holder into the team Richmond would rather not see early in a Super Cup bracket.

Benfica stayed third at 20-16, but the contrast matters. Benfica were a respectable 3-3 in March. Milan were ruthless. Chelsea slid to 19-17 after a 4-4 month, still good enough to remain above the middle but not clean enough to quiet the doubts.

The Middle Tier Starts To Choose

Brighton went 6-1 and sit first in Tier 2 at 27-10. Crystal Palace, despite a 3-4 month, are 26-12 with a plus-11.1 season differential and remain only a game and a half back. Aston Villa had the best pressure month at 5-2, but the actual promotion line still belongs to Brighton and Palace.

The bottom is the sharper story. Atletico Madrid went 1-6 with a minus-96 month and now sit seventh at 15-22. Manchester United remain last at 10-27, but Atletico are the current relegation-line club by standings order and form. That is the sort of sentence a team can hear from across the room.

PSG Lead, Inter Chase, Monaco Complicate

PSG are 30-8 after a 4-2 month with an absurd plus-82 margin. The headline result was a 130-95 win over Monaco, the biggest blowout of the sim and a reminder that Kelly Tripucka's scoring has a team context behind it. Inter Milan, though, went 5-2 and carry a W5 streak into April.

Monaco's month was stranger: 6-3 with a negative margin. The record keeps them on the official Tier 3 promotion line at 23-15, but the underlying shape remains awkward. Barcelona are 23-14 and still dangerous. The one-promotion rule is not built for comfort.

March leaves the league with fewer mysteries, which is not the same as fewer problems. Richmond have control, Milan have belief, Brighton and Palace have the promotion seats, and the Supercup regular-season schedule has handed several clubs a second scoreboard to fear.
