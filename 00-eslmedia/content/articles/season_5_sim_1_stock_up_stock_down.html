November 1985 Stock Report: FL Fart Rise, Manchester United Vanish

The first month rewarded three clubs with repeatable strengths and exposed three others whose isolated good nights cannot cover the scale of their losses.

Damon CrossNovember 1985

We waited through training camp, preseason and every reassuring sentence about “working through combinations.” November has arrived. The standings count now, and some teams discovered that official basketball is a rude editor.

Stock is not the table. It is the distance between what a club was supposed to be and what the first month actually revealed. That is why a third-place team can rise, a seventh-place team can offer one seductive result and still fall, and Manchester United can turn a bad start into something closer to disappearance.

Stock Up: FL Fart Have An Actual Promotion Case

Yes, laugh at the name. Do it quickly. FL Fart went 7-3 in Sim 1, outscored opponents by 77 and beat Bayern Munich by 34. Larry Drew is running the operation at 25 points and 10.9 assists per game. This is no novelty act living on one ridiculous shooting night.

The objection is the road. Six of their seven full-season wins came at home, and Inter Milan and Ajax still occupy the official promotion places. Fine. That makes December a test, not November a trick.

Stock Up: Richmond Do Not Need Your Preseason Vote

AFC Richmond opened 7-2 with a plus-72 margin. They won the one-point game at Benfica, destroyed Chelsea by 44 and kept winning even when the offense was merely good. Michael Cooper is the roster's highest-rated piece; the team's real advantage is that no single performance must carry the entire case.

AC Milan beat them by 22. That result should bother Richmond. It should bother everybody else more that the leader absorbed its worst night and still finished with the tier's strongest record and margin.

Stock Up: Benfica Found A Second Gear

Benfica were seventh on the preseason board. They are 6-3, plus-47 and closed November with three straight wins. Moses Malone is producing 33.9 points, 11.4 rebounds and 2.9 blocks, which is not a supporting detail; it is an explanation for why bad possessions rarely become empty stretches.

The road record is 1-2. That is the bill waiting in December. For now, a club expected to chase the middle is one game from first.

Stock Down: Atletico's Upset Cannot Hide Five Losses

Atletico Madrid beat Inter Milan 123-112 on the road. Wonderful. They lost their other five Sim 1 games and sit 1-6 with the only relegation place in Tier 2. Johnny Moore can create chaos—10.9 assists, 3.3 steals—but chaos is not an offense when the opponent gets the last word every other night.

The upset is the counterargument and the indictment. Atletico can reach the required level. They simply visited it once.

Stock Down: Bayern's One Great Win Made The Rest Look Worse

Bayern Munich beat Sheffield United 109-79. Then came four straight losses, a minus-71 Sim 1 margin and a 43-point collapse at Inter. Tree Rollins can defend the paint; no center can erase every clean look created before the ball reaches him.

Bayern are only half a game above relegation. The problem is no longer that the season started poorly. It is that the evidence already has a shape.

Stock Down: Manchester United Lost By A Month

United's six Sim 1 games produced a minus-94 margin. Barcelona beat them by 52. Their full-season scoring average is 95.1, almost nine points lower than the next-worst Tier 3 offense, and the defense is not good enough to make austerity charming.

They did beat Tottenham Hotspur 104-98. Keep the tape. Frame the box score. Then explain the other five games, because one upset cannot occupy the enormous space between 95 points scored and 114 allowed.

FL Fart made the joke obsolete. Manchester United made the scoreboard feel like an intervention.

December will expose whether the risers own sustainable systems or merely first-month advantages. The fallers have a less elegant assignment: stop asking us to remember the one good night.
