Preseason Sim 1 Review: Villa Made The Loudest Entrance

Aston Villa crushed the opening examination, Richmond and Ajax looked ready immediately, and two winless starts made the preseason harder to dismiss.

Graham TrentSeason 2 - Preseason Sim 1

Preseason is supposed to offer plausible deniability. A club can be experimenting, protecting legs, testing combinations, or simply refusing to reveal anything useful before the real work begins. That argument becomes harder to make when Aston Villa finish their first examination 5-1 with a plus-96 margin and beat Tottenham Hotspur by 35 points.

This was not a table-setting sim in the gentle sense. It produced three convincing early pace-setters, several promotion-level questions, and two clubs already needing the reassurance that none of this counts yet.

Villa did not warm into it

Aston Villa were the sharpest side anywhere in the ESL. They beat Tottenham twice, defeated Manchester City and Paris Saint-Germain, and ended with the best margin in the league. Their lone blemish, an eight-point loss at Barcelona, reads more like useful evidence of mortality than a meaningful interruption.

The Conference League is too crowded to award anything in October, especially in preseason. Still, Villa have established the first standard. Arsenal matched the 5-1 record and deserve their own respect, but Villa's plus-96 makes the distinction straightforward: one side won often, the other won as if it had something to prove.

Richmond supplied the Tier 1 answer

AFC Richmond opened at 5-1 and plus-61, with wins over Bayern Munich, Benfica, Atletico Madrid and Crystal Palace. Their only loss came against AC Milan, a respectable interruption in a schedule that provided enough credible tests to take the performance seriously.

Real Madrid and Milan both reached 4-2 and both had strong stretches, but Richmond were the only Champions League side that combined the best record with repeated wins over rivals expected to matter. It is only the first read, not a declaration of control. It is nevertheless a very clean first read.

Ajax looked most ready for the climb

The Europa League already appears less interested in patience. Ajax finished 5-1 with a plus-51 differential, beating Manchester United twice and adding wins over Brighton, Chelsea and Monaco. Their only defeat was a four-point loss at Juventus, exactly the kind of result that strengthens the sense of a promotion fight rather than weakening Ajax's case.

Brighton and Juventus both went 4-2, while Marseille's strange 2-4 record arrived with a positive point differential. There will be room for movement once results matter. For now, Ajax gave the division its clearest opening argument.

The rough starts are impossible to hide

Crystal Palace lost all six games in Tier 1 and were outscored by 87. Tottenham went 1-5 in Tier 3 with a minus-92 margin, including both heavy defeats to Villa. Neither club has lost a point that matters, and both can reasonably point to the word preseason. What they cannot do is pretend the tape is neutral.

Chelsea also leave the first sim with questions after a 1-5 start in the Europa League. Their one useful intervention, a win over FL Fart, at least gives them something tangible to bring into the next round of preparation.

Preseason results do not place anyone on a promotion or relegation line. They do reveal which teams have already made the questions uncomfortable.
What carries forward

The official season has not started, so there is no legitimate promotion race, relegation panic, or awards ballot yet. What exists is a first map of pressure. Richmond will be expected to confirm their balance. Ajax must show this was more than early readiness. Arsenal now know that Villa have placed an enormous marker in their division.

And for the teams that spent Sim 1 losing heavily, the next preseason appearance has already changed purpose. It is no longer only preparation. It is reassurance.
