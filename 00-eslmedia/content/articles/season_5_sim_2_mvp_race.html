December 1985 MVP Race: Moses Malone Wins The Hard Question

Kiki owns the wildest scoring line, Carroll owns the loudest two-way burden, and Malone owns the production that most clearly changes a winning season.

Damon CrossDecember 1985

Kiki Vandeweghe is averaging 38.7 points on 70.2 percent true shooting. That is not a typo. It is not a video-game setting. It is the kind of number that usually ends an MVP argument before the other side has cleared its throat.

Except Brighton are 7-8. Joe Barry Carroll is giving AC Milan 35 points and 3.7 blocks, except Milan are 6-11. Moses Malone is giving Benfica 31.9 points, 11.2 rebounds and 3.6 blocks while they sit second at 11-6. There is the hard question: how much extraordinary production should survive a losing record when another extraordinary player is turning his minutes into wins?

My answer is Malone. Not because winning erases every statistical gap, and not because Vandeweghe's season is somehow empty. Malone leads because his individual case remains elite after the team context is applied instead of requiring the team context to be ignored.

Tier 1 Ballot
The Leader: Moses Malone
  1. 1Moses Malone, Benfica
    31.9 PTS | 11.2 REB | 3.6 BLK | 59.6 TS% | +8.4
    STAYED NO. 1. Benfica are 11-6 because their best player controls both ends without surrendering volume. Malone's scoring is not Kiki-level absurd, but it comes with elite rebounding, rim protection and the strongest on-court margin of the three leading cases. That is value with no missing chapter.
  2. 2Kiki Vandeweghe, Brighton
    38.7 PTS | 5.8 REB | 70.2 TS% | 30.4 PER | +3.7
    UP FROM NO. 3. Thirty-eight points on seventy percent true shooting is offensive nonsense in the best possible way. Brighton's positive margin keeps this from becoming empty-calorie slander. The 7-8 record keeps it from No. 1. If Brighton cross into the top half, this ballot may become impossible to defend.
  3. 3Joe Barry Carroll, AC Milan
    35.0 PTS | 7.7 REB | 3.7 BLK | 26.0 PER | -3.5
    DOWN FROM NO. 2. Carroll is carrying a burden nobody should envy: be the offense, protect the rim and watch the team lose his minutes anyway. The negative plus-minus and 6-11 record are not entirely his fault. MVP is not an innocence hearing. Milan need his dominance to change outcomes.
  4. 4Tom Chambers, AFC Richmond
    31.1 PTS | 6.5 REB | 63.8 TS% | 25.0 PER | 12-4 TEAM
    UP ONE. Chambers is the best scorer on the best Tier 1 team, which sounds like a winning argument until the first three stat lines arrive. His efficiency and team record close much of the production gap. Sharing Richmond's credit with Michael Cooper and a deeper structure keeps him just outside the leading trio.
  5. 5Sidney Moncrief, Real Madrid
    31.3 PTS | 6.1 REB | 4.9 AST | 1.8 STL | W5 TEAM
    NEW TO TOP FIVE. Real have won five straight and Moncrief is the reason their best possessions work at both ends. The 57.5 true-shooting mark trails this ballot's scoring monsters, but his creation and point-of-attack defense give the streak a shape more durable than simple shot-making.
Tier 2 Ballot
The Leader: Larry Nance
  1. 1Larry Nance, Aston Villa
    30.0 PTS | 11.5 REB | 2.2 BLK | 62.2 TS% | W10 TEAM
    UP FROM NO. 2. Villa went 8-0 in December and have won ten straight. Nance gives that run its repeatable centre: efficient scoring, elite rebounding and real rim protection. He does not have the tier's largest raw line. He has the line most responsible for changing the promotion race.
  2. 2Andre Iguodala, Ajax
    27.6 PTS | 5.1 REB | 4.1 AST | 2.2 STL | 66.2 TS%
    UP THREE. Ajax own the tier's best winning percentage and plus-181 margin, and Iguodala's plus-9.9 on-court mark is the cleanest bridge between star and team. His case lacks Nance's rebounding burden and December theatre. It may be the more sustainable profile.
  3. 3Steve Johnson, AC Sparta Praha
    35.6 PTS | 11.3 REB | 1.6 BLK | 63.3 TS% | 28.6 PER
    DOWN FROM NO. 1. Johnson remains the tier's most overwhelming individual producer. AC Sparta Praha remain 7-10 and went minus-93 in December. At some point the ballot must ask whether a player's dominance changes the environment around him. Right now, it changes the score more than the season.
  4. 4Robert Parish, Marseille
    27.9 PTS | 14.4 REB | 2.2 BLK | 22.9 PER | 0.0 +/-
    STAYED NO. 4. Fourteen rebounds a night give Parish a physical claim nobody else on this ballot matches. Marseille's 6-8 record and neutral on-court margin tell the uncomfortable half: the dominance has stabilized them without lifting them into the promotion group.
  5. 5Larry Drew, FL Fart
    22.7 PTS | 10.1 AST | 1.7 STL | +6.3 | 11-7 TEAM
    STAYED NO. 5. Drew's shooting efficiency is ordinary; his control is not. Ten assists a night organize one of the tier's strongest offenses, and the positive on-court margin survives his missed shots. FL Fart's road weakness keeps a genuine floor-raising season from climbing higher.
Tier 3 Ballot
The Leader: Bernard King
  1. 1Bernard King, Barcelona
    30.4 PTS | 6.5 REB | 4.1 AST | 65.7 TS% | +8.1
    UP FROM NO. 2. Barcelona are three games clear and King is their hardest possession to solve. He combines elite volume with elite efficiency and a strong on-court margin. In a one-promotion tier, the best player on the team creating separation gets the first line of the ballot.
  2. 2Chris Bosh, Arsenal
    27.9 PTS | 9.0 REB | 2.0 BLK | 68.9 TS% | 27.0 PER
    UP THREE. Bosh may own the best two-way efficiency case in Tier 3. Arsenal went 5-2 in December and moved into the central chase. The only reason this is not No. 1 is distance: Barcelona have four more wins and King is producing at the same level for the team everyone must catch.
  3. 3Steve Kerr, Tottenham Hotspur
    25.9 PTS | 4.1 AST | 1.9 STL | 52.4 3P% | 66.1 TS%
    UP ONE. Kerr's shooting changes defensive geometry before he touches the ball, and he supplied part of the formula that beat Barcelona. Tottenham went 3-5 in December, so the ballot cannot treat one brilliant matchup as a season. The individual gravity is real; the team lift is inconsistent.
  4. 4Andre Drummond, Monaco
    23.8 PTS | 13.5 REB | 2.0 BLK | 21.7 PER | -1.9
    DOWN ONE. Drummond remains the tier's strongest possession-finisher and rebounder. Monaco have won three straight, but his negative on-court margin and the team's even record keep the case below players whose production is bending the table more clearly.
  5. 5Otis Thorpe, Valencia
    25.9 PTS | 8.7 REB | 66.1 TS% | +1.1 | 9-8 TEAM
    NEW TO TOP FIVE. Thorpe scores with extraordinary economy and gives Valencia a reliable interior answer beside Isiah Thomas. A 4-5 month and two-game losing streak cap the rise. The production says contender; December says the team has not decided whether to agree.
The MVP is not automatically the best player on the best team. He is the player whose excellence leaves the fewest unanswered questions about why that team wins.

January's pressure is obvious. Vandeweghe must drag Brighton over .500. Carroll must make Milan's star minutes profitable. Iguodala can take Tier 2 if Ajax keep separating while Villa cool. Bosh can force the hardest Tier 3 comparison if Arsenal close the standings gap. Malone, Nance and King lead today because production and consequence currently meet in the same place.
