December 1985 Tier 3 Power Rankings: Barcelona Create Distance

The leader turned one promotion place into a three-game chase, while Arsenal and Manchester City finally made their strongest ideas survive the schedule.

Nina ValeDecember 1985

Tier 3 offers one promotion place, so trust must include distance from Barcelona as well as belief in the team itself. December made that standard harsher. The leader went 6-2. Two challengers surged. Neither gained a game.

Barcelona remain first by a clear margin. Arsenal and Manchester City make the largest upward moves because both combined positive full-season evidence with excellent December form. The middle is still crowded; the top is not.

Tier 3 Trust Board — December 1985
  1. 1BarcelonaSTAYED NO. 1
    12-4 overall | 6-2 December | +198 | W6 | Paul Pierce

    Barcelona own the best record, best margin and the only promotion place. Bernard King's 30.4 points on 65.7 percent true shooting give the offense a brutal first option; Paul Pierce makes the second action nearly as efficient. Tottenham beat them by 12. Barcelona then won six straight, including a 33-point answer against Valencia. The concern exists. The response already arrived.

  2. 2ArsenalUP 4 FROM NO. 6
    8-6 | 5-2 December | +60 | W1 | Chris Bosh

    Chris Bosh is averaging 27.9 points, nine rebounds and two blocks on 68.9 percent true shooting. December finally gave that production a team shape: five wins, plus-64 and a 21-point victory over Monaco. Arsenal are three games back because November counts. They rank second because the current defense and star efficiency offer the clearest route to making those games matter.

  3. 3Manchester CityUP 4 FROM NO. 7
    9-7 | 6-2 December | +32 | W3 | Luol Deng

    City's preseason promise finally entered the official record. Six December wins, a plus-56 margin and a three-game close deserve a four-place jump. The awkward fact is a one-point loss to Manchester United, evidence that the offense can still flatten at the wrong moment. The stronger fact is that City stopped allowing such moments to define the month.

  4. 4ValenciaDOWN 2 FROM NO. 2
    9-8 | 4-5 December | +31 | L2 | Brian Shaw

    Valencia delivered the month's largest win, 136-94 over Juventus, and still fell because the surrounding evidence weakened. Barcelona beat them by 33, the month finished below .500 and the road schedule remains unstable. Isiah Thomas creates 11.5 assists a night and Otis Thorpe scores efficiently. The parts remain credible; the direction does not.

  5. 5MonacoSTAYED NO. 5
    8-8 | 4-3 December | -26 | W3 | Andre Drummond

    Monaco's three-game streak includes a 94-61 suffocation of Manchester United, the kind of defensive result a neutral-margin team can build upon. Andre Drummond supplies 23.8 points and 13.5 rebounds. The minus-26 season margin and 21-point loss at Arsenal explain why the board needs more than a strong finish before moving them into the chase.

  6. 6Tottenham HotspurDOWN 2 FROM NO. 4
    7-9 | 3-5 December | -32 | L1 | Hubert Davis

    Tottenham own a valuable contradiction: they beat Barcelona 114-102 and still lost the month by 41. Steve Kerr's shooting can alter a matchup and the home record remains positive. A 2-6 road record prevents that solution from travelling. The leader win deserves memory, not immunity.

  7. 7JuventusDOWN 4 FROM NO. 3
    6-9 | 2-6 December | -45 | L3 | Larry Smith

    Juventus beat Valencia by 19 and later lost the return game by 42. That swing captures the month: a defense capable of producing control at home and incapable of surviving travel. They are 6-2 in their own building and 0-7 away. Seven places on a trust board cannot be built from one address.

  8. 8Manchester UnitedSTAYED NO. 8
    4-12 | 2-7 December | -218 | L6 | J.J. Redick

    United's one-point derby win over City is the upset a bottom team should use to alter its month. They followed it with six straight losses and scored 61 at Monaco. J.J. Redick provides shooting but not enough creation to repair the league's weakest offense. The next trigger is modest: connect two competitive games before discussing movement.

Board Notes
  • Control team: Barcelona, three games clear with a plus-198 margin.
  • Biggest risers: Arsenal and Manchester City, each finally pairing form with season evidence.
  • Trust problem: Valencia's direction after a losing month.
  • Panic team: Manchester United, six straight losses and minus-218 overall.

The next board changes only if Barcelona participate. Arsenal and City can keep winning and remain three games back if the leader does the same. That is the particular cruelty of a one-place race: improvement is necessary, but somebody else's weakness is required.
