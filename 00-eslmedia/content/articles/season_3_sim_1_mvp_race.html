MVP Race - Season 3 - Sim 1

November 1983 MVP Race: Carroll, Kiki And Purvis Open The Ballot

Joe Barry Carroll, Kiki Vandeweghe and Purvis Short headline the first November MVP ladders across the three ESL tiers.

Damon CrossSeason 3 - Sim 1

The first MVP board is not a coronation. It is a warning label. Joe Barry Carroll is blocking everything in sight, Kiki Vandeweghe is scoring like he misplaced the brakes, and Purvis Short has Monaco turning a forgotten preseason slot into a live Tier 3 claim.

Production gets a player into the room. Team context decides how long he stays there. The early ballot rewards volume, efficiency, availability and whether the numbers are dragging a club toward something meaningful.

Tier 1 MVP Ladder
  1. 1Joe Barry Carroll
    AC Milan | 30.7 pts, 9.4 reb, 1.9 ast | 5.8 blk
    Carroll starts first because 30 a night with nearly six blocks is not normal box-score weather. Milan are only 5-4, so the case is not fully protected by team dominance, but his two-way footprint is the loudest individual file in Tier 1.
  2. 2Adrian Dantley
    Benfica | 31.3 pts, 7.2 reb, 5.9 ast | +6.6
    Dantley has the cleaner team argument than Carroll and a more complete offensive line than most of the field. The reason he is second is positional impact: Carroll's rim control changes games in a way Dantley's scoring pressure has to keep matching every night.
  3. 3Tom Chambers
    AFC Richmond | 31.6 pts, 6.2 reb | +13.3
    Chambers has the best team context on the board. Richmond are unbeaten, and his plus-minus tells the same story as the standings. The caveat is playmaking and defensive range; the case is brilliant, but slightly narrower than the two above him.
  4. 4Jim Paxson
    Chelsea | 34.5 pts, 4.7 reb, 3.7 ast | +4.0
    Paxson has the scoring number that makes people stop scrolling. Chelsea being 3-3 keeps the argument in the second row for now, but if the team rises, he has the kind of clean scoring profile that can jump quickly.
  5. 5Bernard King
    Bayern Munich | 31.6 pts, 5.9 reb, 5.1 ast | -5.9
    King is the uncomfortable inclusion. The production is MVP-grade; the team results are not. A 1-6 Bayern start means every King argument has to carry the phrase "despite the record," and that phrase gets heavy fast.
Tier 2 MVP Ladder
  1. 1Kiki Vandeweghe
    Brighton | 41.6 pts, 8.9 reb, 3.7 ast | +10.1
    Kiki owns the first Tier 2 ballot because 41.6 points per game is an argument with a siren attached. Brighton are on the promotion line, not wandering below it, so this is not empty volume. It is production with consequence.
  2. 2Larry Nance
    Aston Villa | 32.9 pts, 11.2 reb, 2.4 ast | 2.0 blk
    Nance has the best big-man case in the tier: scoring, rebounding, shot blocking and enough team relevance to matter. Villa's uneven finish keeps him second, but his two-way line is the cleanest challenge to Kiki's scoring avalanche.
  3. 3Robert Parish
    Marseille | 25.3 pts, 14.9 reb, 2.8 ast | 1.9 blk
    Parish is carrying a hard job: Marseille's record is below .500, but the four-game winning streak gives his rebounding and interior scoring a fresh context. If Marseille keep moving, Parish's case gets serious quickly.
  4. 4Rolando Blackman
    Juventus | 30.7 pts, 4.6 reb, 3.3 ast | +4.9
    Blackman is the clean scorer on a team hovering near the promotion chase. He does not have Kiki's absurd volume or Nance's size of impact, but Juventus are close enough that a hot fortnight could pull him into the top three.
  5. 5Reggie Theus
    Crystal Palace | 17.5 pts, 9.1 ast | +13.0
    Theus is the context candidate. His raw scoring will not win a poster contest, but Palace are 9-1 and his passing is part of why the league's loudest team looks organized instead of merely hot.
Tier 3 MVP Ladder
  1. 1Purvis Short
    Monaco | 30.9 pts, 6.4 reb, 3.1 ast | +6.7
    Short gets the first Tier 3 slot because Monaco are 6-1 and his scoring is the cleanest star explanation for the jump. The preseason board did not see this coming. The November ballot has to.
  2. 2Kelly Tripucka
    Paris Saint-Germain | 29.8 pts, 5.0 reb, 4.0 ast | +4.9
    Tripucka is close because PSG have direct-result credibility against the top group and his line travels across matchups. He trails Short because PSG have already played three more losses into the file.
  3. 3T.R. Dunn
    Monaco | 20.0 pts, 9.1 reb, 4.6 ast | +9.7
    Dunn is not the loudest scorer, but he might be the glue candidate of the whole tier. Twenty points, nine boards and real passing on a 6-1 team is not a side note. It is why Monaco's rise feels less fluky.
  4. 4Lewis Lloyd
    Sporting CP | 22.3 pts, 5.9 reb, 2.9 ast
    Lloyd is here because Sporting's plus differential says the team is better than its record, and his scoring keeps them competitive in games they have not learned to finish. He needs wins more than numbers now.
  5. 5World B. Free
    Inter Milan | 27.9 pts, 3.6 reb, 3.0 ast | +7.6
    World B. Free has the veteran scorer case on a team with the best differential in the top-four Tier 3 pack. He sits fifth only because the race is crowded and Inter's slide from the top board cost him some first-month shine.
This is the first ballot, not the final word. But Carroll, Kiki and Purvis made everyone else chase them.

December will tell us who has a real race and who just had a loud November. The fastest path up the board is not another 30-point night; it is a 30-point night that moves the standings.
