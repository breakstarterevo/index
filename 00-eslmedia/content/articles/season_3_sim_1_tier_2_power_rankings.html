November 1983 Tier 2 Power Rankings: Palace Make Promotion Feel Real

Crystal Palace turn a 9-1 November and plus-157 margin into the clearest promotion case on the board.

Nina ValeSeason 3 - Sim 1

Tier 2 has one team playing like promotion is an administrative step and seven teams trying to decide whether that is rude or accurate. Crystal Palace stay first because the numbers leave no graceful rebuttal.

Palace are 9-1 with a plus-157 differential. Brighton are dangerous enough to sit second, but Palace have already made the conversation feel less like a race for first and more like a fight for the other promotion seat.

Tier 2 Board - November 1983
  1. 1Crystal Palace
    STAYED NO. 1 | 9-1 | +157 | W8 | Darrell Griffith
    Palace have the tier's best record, best differential, and longest winning streak. The Brighton loss keeps this from being a coronation, but the eight straight wins since then are a pretty strong editorial response. Darrell Griffith gives them burst; Reggie Theus gives the whole thing order.
  2. 2Brighton
    UP 2 FROM NO. 4 | 5-2 | +79 | W3 | Kiki Vandeweghe
    Brighton have the best individual scoring argument in the tier with Kiki Vandeweghe, and the 43-point flattening of Ajax made the promotion case impossible to miss. The United loss is the annoying receipt. It stops Brighton from being 1B to Palace and leaves them as the strongest chaser.
  3. 3Juventus
    UP 2 FROM NO. 5 | 4-3 | +25 | W1 | Rolando Blackman
    Juventus are not loud, but they are coherent. Rolando Blackman gives them a scorer who can survive a bad possession diet, and the plus-25 margin says the 4-3 record has more substance than decoration. The next jump requires beating the teams currently sitting on the line.
  4. 4Aston Villa
    DOWN 2 FROM NO. 2 | 4-4 | +18 | L2 | Bill Laimbeer
    Villa's fall is about timing. The full file is still playable, and Bill Laimbeer keeps the interior argument stable, but closing November with two losses makes every earlier positive feel a little less useful. They are still in the chase. They are no longer controlling it.
  5. 5Atletico Madrid
    UP 2 FROM NO. 7 | 4-4 | -38 | W1 | James Donaldson
    Atletico are the least comfortable .500 team on the board. The Ajax win helps, and James Donaldson gives them a real base, but a minus-38 differential is why they sit behind Villa despite matching the record. The results say alive. The margins say careful.
  6. 6Marseille
    STAYED NO. 6 | 4-5 | -51 | W4 | Robert Parish
    Marseille are weird in a useful way: bad differential, losing record, four straight wins. Robert Parish is keeping them credible while the table catches up to the current form. If the streak survives another week, this ranking will look too low.
  7. 7Manchester United
    UP 1 FROM NO. 8 | 2-7 | -90 | L6 | Robert Reid
    United get credit for the Brighton upset and almost nothing else. The six-game losing streak has turned that result into a strange artifact rather than a foundation. They are above Ajax because the head wound is slightly less deep, not because the room is calm.
  8. 8Ajax
    DOWN 5 FROM NO. 3 | 1-7 | -100 | L6 | Cliff Robinson
    Ajax are the panic case. Cliff Robinson can keep the box score interesting, but one win and a minus-100 differential make the rest of the conversation brutally short. The 43-point Brighton loss is not just a bad night. It is the image of the month.
Board Notes
Control Team: Crystal Palace | Biggest Riser: Brighton | Trust Problem: Aston Villa | Panic Team: Ajax

Palace have separated from the tier. Brighton and Juventus are the clearest promotion threats behind them. Marseille are the form team worth watching; Ajax are already fighting to keep the season from tilting into survival mode.

Palace are not just first. They are forcing the rest of Tier 2 to argue for second.

The next board turns on whether Palace finally show a wobble and whether Brighton can keep enough pressure on the line to make promotion feel like a two-team claim instead of a Palace announcement.
