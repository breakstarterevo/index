November Tier 3 Power Rankings: Villa Keep The Promotion Seat Hot

Aston Villa lead a tight Tier 3 promotion race, with Arsenal, Inter Milan and Paris Saint-Germain close enough to matter immediately.

Nina ValeNovember 1982

Tier 3 is already the most compressed promotion argument in the league. Aston Villa have the line, Arsenal have the same win total, Inter Milan own the best late push, and Paris Saint-Germain have enough scoring to complicate every projection.

The order starts with the line, but it does not end there. Margin and momentum matter because only one team goes up.

1. Aston Villa - 5-1, plus-73

Previous board: level at No. 1. Villa have the cleanest promotion claim because they combine the best record with the best point differential in the tier. Bill Laimbeer gives them a strong interior floor, and the W3 finish matters in a race where there is no room to coast.

2. Arsenal - 5-2, plus-35

Previous board: no change. Arsenal are not behind by much. Buck Williams has been efficient and physical, and a 5-2 start keeps the pressure directly on Villa. The reason they sit second is margin: Villa have created more separation in fewer losses.

3. Inter Milan - 4-3, plus-42

Previous board: up. Inter are only one game back and their plus-42 differential is better than Sporting's. World B. Free gives them the burst scorer a promotion chase needs, and the 108-66 win over Valencia was a reminder that Inter can make a game disappear quickly.

4. Paris Saint-Germain - 4-2, plus-36

Previous board: up into the live group. PSG have the awkwardly strong case: fewer games, strong margin, and Joe Barry Carroll plus Kelly Tripucka giving them enough offense to make the record feel real. The only reason they are fourth is that the teams above them have already supplied more total table pressure.

5. Barcelona - 3-4, minus-15

Previous board: down. Otis Birdsong and Jalen Duren give Barcelona interesting player-level reasons to believe, but the team result is not there yet. A losing record in a one-promotion tier means the next month has to be about urgency, not patience.

6. Tottenham Hotspur - 2-3, minus-30

Previous board: slight rise from the basement. Darryl Dawkins keeps Tottenham from being dismissed, and the schedule volume means their record is still incomplete. The downside is that a minus-30 margin leaves little room for optimism unless the next run produces clearer wins.

7. Manchester City - 2-6, minus-75

Previous board: down. Calvin Natt has an MVP-stat case and Allen Leavell gives City a steady guard, but the team is 2-6. That split between individual production and team result is the whole problem.

8. Valencia - 1-5, minus-66

Previous board: bottom. Isiah Thomas has already shown why Valencia can become more interesting later. November did not let that promise breathe. The 42-point loss at Inter Milan left the table saying the same thing as the eye test.

Villa still hold the promotion seat, but this tier is close enough that one uneven week could rewrite the top four.

The next month should be judged by pressure. Villa need to defend the line. Sporting need to turn proximity into control. Inter and PSG need to prove their margins can become a promotion case rather than a warning to the leaders.
