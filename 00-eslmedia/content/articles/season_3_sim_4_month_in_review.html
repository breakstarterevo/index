February 1984 Review: Richmond Close The Door And PSG Shake The Room

Richmond made Tier 1 feel settled, PSG made the ECL race feel heavier, and February left several clubs with fewer excuses than before.

Graham TrentSeason 3 - Sim 4

There are months that rearrange a league, and there are months that simply remove the softer arguments. February did more of the second. AFC Richmond went 6-0, won by 14.2 points per game, and treated the top tier less like a race than a matter waiting to be filed.

The signature was not only the record. Richmond beat Bayern Munich by 32, handled Chelsea, and closed by taking Benfica 131-116. Tom Chambers and Magic Johnson give the front of the table enough scoring and orchestration to make the unbeaten month feel sturdy rather than theatrical.

PSG Make The Loudest Noise

Paris Saint-Germain were the month finest team by volume. An 8-1 February with a plus-116 differential would have been enough on its own, but the 132-102 win over Inter Milan changed the tone of the Conference League race. Kelly Tripucka remains the lead headline, while Brad Davis keeps giving the attack order.

The only PSG blemish was also the strangest turn of the month: Tottenham Hotspur, still near the bottom, stole a 106-105 upset. It did not knock PSG from first, but it kept the one-promotion race from becoming completely airless.

Palace Win The Weirdest Split Decision

Crystal Palace only went 3-3, yet they beat Atletico Madrid by 42 and still sit first in Tier 2 at 23-8. Brighton were better by record, Ajax were hotter by month, and Juventus stayed close enough to remain irritating. Palace, though, still own the actual table.

That distinction matters. Brighton hold the second promotion place at 21-9. Ajax went 7-1 and still sits sixth, a reminder that a fine month can improve the mood without moving the real line all the way to your door.

The Trapdoors Are Less Abstract

Tier 1 has two relegation spots, and the bottom two remain Real Madrid and Sheffield United. Real Madrid are 10-20, Sheffield are 9-20, and Bayern Munich are close enough at 11-19 to avoid comfort. In Tier 2, Manchester United are still last, while Marseille sit one place above after a 2-6 month that offered very little polish.

Sporting CP had the coldest month in the league at 1-5, closing on a four-game slide. They are not the bottom of Tier 3, but February made their profile feel less like a middling annoyance and more like a club running out of clean answers.

Next month starts with a simple question: can anyone make the top lines move, or are we now watching the contenders defend what February made obvious?
