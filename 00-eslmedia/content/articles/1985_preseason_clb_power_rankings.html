1985 CLB Preseason Power Rankings: Brighton Look Ready, Milan Look Late

Brighton's two-way control earns the first seat, but Richmond's offence and Palace's depth ensure that CLB begins with pressure at both ends of the table.

Nina Vale1985 Preseason - CLB

The first CLB board measures trust, not exhibition wins alone. Record matters, average scoring margin matters more, and the way a roster produced both matters most. Preseason opponents do not defend every action with regular-season precision, so shooting spikes receive less weight than repeatable creation, rebounding and lineup flexibility.

CLB's consequence is direct: two relegation places turn every early weakness into a real source of pressure. Brighton enters with the cleanest evidence. AC Milan enters with a star scoring 35 points a night and almost nothing about the team feeling comfortable.

1985 CLB Preseason Trust BoardEight Clubs
1BrightonPRESEASON ENTRY
4-1 · +13.6 margin · Kiki Vandeweghe 30.8 PPG · Rickey Green 10.2 APG

Brighton owns the best CLB combination of result and method. Vandeweghe scored efficiently without monopolising minutes, Green organised the offence and Jack Sikma controlled enough of the glass to keep the shape intact. Kurt Nimphius adds a disruptive second centre rather than redundant depth. The concern is whether a five-game sample made the defence look tidier than it is. The first regular-season test is simple: can Brighton maintain control when Vandeweghe's 62.4 percent shooting becomes human?

2AFC RichmondPRESEASON ENTRY
4-2 · +4.5 margin · Tom Chambers 33.2 PPG · Rick Mahorn 10.3 RPG

Richmond remains easier to trust than its margin suggests because Chambers still creates the tier's most reliable scoring pressure. Michael Adams supplied 6.8 assists per game, an important development with Magic Johnson no longer carrying the same physical profile. Yet the defence allowed 113.3 per night. Richmond can win exchanges at that temperature; it should not volunteer to live there. The next trigger is whether Adams' larger role reduces the strain on Magic without flattening the transition game.

3Crystal PalacePRESEASON ENTRY
4-1 · +3.2 margin · Alex English 24.4 PPG · Reggie Theus 9.4 APG

Palace scored 121.2 points per game and occasionally defended as if entertainment value carried a standings bonus. Theus, English and Darrell Griffith can overwhelm a normal rotation, while Rolando Blackman's improvement gives Palace another scorer it has not yet found enough minutes for. A 118.0 defensive allowance is not a footnote. Palace sits third because the offensive depth is real and the defensive indifference may become expensive as soon as opponents stop experimenting.

4Paris Saint-GermainPRESEASON ENTRY
3-3 · +4.0 margin · Dan Roundfield 24.0 PPG · Steve Nash 8.2 APG

PSG's even record hides a positive scoring margin and a useful shift toward Roundfield and Mike Gminski inside. Nash still moved the ball despite his camp decline, and Kelly Tripucka contributed without looking like the untouchable centre of every possession. The unresolved issue is hierarchy: Dan Majerle barely appeared, Birdsong declined and the old wing order no longer fits neatly. PSG has enough production to stay above the danger conversation, provided it chooses current roles rather than historic ones.

5ChelseaPRESEASON ENTRY
3-3 · -1.5 margin · Jim Paxson 33.8 PPG · Ray Williams 8.2 APG

Paxson's scoring makes Chelsea dangerous on any night, and Mark Aguirre supplied 7.3 rebounds around a roster still learning its new distribution. The awkward number belongs to Chet Holmgren: 11.7 points after a difficult development cycle. His 3.7 combined steals and blocks show that he can influence games without scoring, but Chelsea cannot ask Paxson to carry this much half-court creation indefinitely. The board waits for Aguirre to become a co-author rather than an expensive supporting character.

6Real MadridPRESEASON ENTRY
2-4 · -6.5 margin · Sidney Moncrief 34.7 PPG · Allen Leavell 11.8 APG

Madrid produced two of the tier's most impressive individual lines and still lost four times. Moncrief scored with force, Leavell created at an elite rate and Jawann Oldham pulled down 12.7 rebounds per game. The pieces are visible. Their connection is not. Leavell shot 39.5 percent, and the offence too often alternated between his organisation and Moncrief's rescue work. Madrid will rise quickly if those possessions begin to build on each other.

7BenficaPRESEASON ENTRY
2-4 · -5.5 margin · Moses Malone 24.5 PPG · Buck Williams 12.5 RPG

Moses Malone and Buck Williams give Benfica the board's most obvious rebounding foundation. Malone also blocked 4.3 shots per game. The problem begins when possession moves away from the paint. Chucky Atkins led the club with 5.2 assists, a modest supply for two finishers who deserve earlier and cleaner touches. Benfica can survive ugly games; CLB's relegation pressure makes it dangerous to specialise in them.

8AC MilanPRESEASON ENTRY
1-5 · -9.0 margin · Joe Barry Carroll 34.7 PPG · John Lucas 8.7 APG

Carroll averaged 34.7 points, 9.7 rebounds and 3.3 blocks. Milan responded by winning once. Laimbeer's sharp ratings decline appeared in a reduced 14.3-point scoring line, and the defence conceded 119.7 per game. There is too much talent here for eighth to feel permanent, but trust is earned by the team that exists. Milan's immediate test is whether it can redesign the frontcourt relationship before Carroll's excellence becomes another way to describe a loss.

Control Team: Brighton. Highest Ceiling: Crystal Palace. Trust Problem: Real Madrid. Panic Team: AC Milan.
What The Order Does Not Say

This is not a prediction that Milan will finish last or Brighton will cruise through CLB. Established talent narrows the meaning of six exhibition games. Carroll can keep Milan in contests the current defence does not deserve to enter, and Moncrief gives Madrid a direct route out of any early offensive confusion. Those are reasons to expect movement, not reasons to erase what happened.

The middle is particularly unstable. Palace has the deepest scoring collection but the weakest defensive discipline among the top four. PSG's positive margin is more persuasive than its record, yet its rotation remains caught between old status and new evidence. Chelsea owns a premier scorer and an expensive new running mate, but has not shown how those strengths become a complete half-court identity.

Brighton's advantage is that its questions are smaller. It needs shooting regression to remain manageable and Nimphius' defensive rate to settle at merely useful. The clubs behind it need to change who initiates, who defends or who closes. Preseason trust belongs to the team requiring the fewest acts of reinvention.

Brighton's lead can disappear quickly because CLB has too much established scoring for preseason margins to settle the argument. The next board will move on defensive evidence: whether Palace can cool games, whether Richmond can prevent them from becoming track meets and whether Milan can stop asking Carroll to make a broken possession look respectable.
