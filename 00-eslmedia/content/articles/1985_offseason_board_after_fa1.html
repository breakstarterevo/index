After FA1, Four Stars Still Control The 1985 Offseason Board

Nine opening-round deals removed major incumbents from the market, but Griffith, Gervin, Bird and Free leave the remaining star tier remarkably intact.

Nina Vale1985 Offseason - After FA1

FA1 paid nine players and somehow left the four strongest available ratings untouched. Magic Johnson returned to AFC Richmond for $125 million over five years. Sidney Moncrief stayed with Real Madrid for $143.75 million, Kelly Tripucka stayed in Paris for $137.5 million and Larry Nance remained at Aston Villa for $105.66 million. Those were franchise decisions. They did not settle the open market.

Darrell Griffith is still available at age 25 with a 149 overall rating. George Gervin remains one point behind him. Larry Bird still supplies the best premium wing profile, and World B. Free gives every guard-needy bidder another headliner. FA1 removed several clubs' internal emergencies, but it did not reduce the top of this board.

What FA1 Changed

The round rewarded continuity. Johnny Moore re-signed with Atletico Madrid, Cliff Robinson with Ajax, Buck Williams with Benfica and the four stars above with their incumbent clubs. Chelsea made the one major external strike, signing Mark Aguirre for $86 million over four years, while Manchester City paid James Bailey $30.6 million over three. The consequences are positional: Chelsea solved one wing slot, City added frontcourt depth and several teams used major portions of their room without competing for the remaining four stars.

The Post-FA1 Market Board
RankPlayerMarket case
  1. 1Darrell Griffith — SG, 25

    149 overall · 149 potential · last club: Crystal Palace

    The cleanest blend of age, production and positional demand. Crystal Palace has only three guards and room to retain him, but Inter Milan, AC Milan, Aston Villa and Chelsea can all make credible basketball cases. Griffith changes the market because every alternative guard is priced in relation to him.

  2. 2George Gervin — SG, 31

    148 overall · 148 potential · last club: AC Milan

    Age separates him from Griffith, not present quality. A club paying for the next two seasons rather than the next five may prefer the certainty of Gervin's elite scoring. AC Milan has more than $30.6 million in room, but also three broad depth needs; keeping him could consume the flexibility required to repair the rest.

  3. 3Larry Bird — SF, 26

    142 overall · 142 potential · last club: AC Milan

    Bird is the board's premium answer at the position with fewer elite alternatives. That scarcity may matter more than the eight-point overall gap to Griffith. Real Madrid has only one wing, Monaco lists wing depth as a need and AC Milan must decide whether Bird or Gervin is the more difficult player to replace.

  4. 4World B. Free — SG, 28

    141 overall · 141 potential · last club: Inter Milan

    Free is too good to be called a fallback, yet the two guards above him create exactly that negotiating danger. Inter owns the league's largest cap figure and needs guards. It can pursue the top of the board, retain Free, or use the existence of both options to resist an inflated opening demand.

  5. 5Greg Ballard — SF, 28

    134 overall · 135 potential · last club: Inter Milan

    The first major pressure release on the board. A club that loses Bird can still acquire a starting wing without dropping into a developmental tier. Ballard's market becomes most interesting if Inter prioritises its backcourt and allows another bidder to turn him into the best available player at his position.

  6. 6Jack Sikma — C, 26

    130 overall · 130 potential · last club: Chelsea

    Sikma ranks sixth in raw overall but may possess a top-three market. The next centre on the overall board is Bill Cartwright at 121. Brighton and Aston Villa both have frontcourt needs and more than $25 million in cap room. One early bid from either club could make Chelsea pay a scarcity premium to keep its best big.

  7. 7Allen Leavell — PG, 26

    126 overall · 126 potential · last club: AC Milan

    Leavell is the first true point guard on the board and therefore more valuable than his overall rank suggests. Inter, Chelsea and Sporting CP all show guard scarcity. His price will reveal whether those teams distinguish between adding another scorer and adding someone who can organise the roster already in place.

  8. 8John Long — SF, 26

    125 overall · 135 potential · last club: FL Fart

    No player in this range offers a larger gap between present rating and potential. Long is not the safe wing choice; Ballard and Robert Reid own stronger current cases. He is the swing for a team prepared to pay for what the next contract might contain rather than what the last season proved.

  9. 9Rickey Green — PG, 28

    125 overall · 125 potential · last club: Chelsea

    Green offers the opposite proposition to Long: known quality, no projection required and immediate control of a thin backcourt. If Leavell signs quickly, Green's leverage rises. If the headline shooting guards linger, clubs may try to solve creation and scoring in one transaction instead.

  10. 10Robert Reid — SF, 27

    124 overall · 124 potential · last club: Atletico Madrid

    Reid is the practical wing: enough present value to start, no speculative development cost and a fit on almost any roster lacking size on the perimeter. His ceiling is not the attraction. Avoiding the sharp drop after the first wave is.

  11. 11Gerald Henderson — PG, 27

    122 overall · 123 potential · last club: Juventus

    Henderson sits in the useful middle of the point-guard market. He will not set the opening price, but a team that waits out Leavell and Green can still buy functional prime-age minutes rather than accept a roster compromise.

  12. 12Norm Nixon — SG, 27

    122 overall · 122 potential · last club: Chelsea

    Nixon closes the first dozen because the league has money and too many teams need guards for the position to collapse completely. He is also a warning against confusing a deep class with unlimited depth: once this tier moves, the clean starter options narrow quickly.

Control player: Darrell Griffith. Scarcity play: Jack Sikma. Best alternative route: Greg Ballard. Volatility bet: John Long.
What Can Break The Order

The strongest counterargument to Griffith at No. 1 is positional scarcity. Bird is clearly separated from the next wing, while Sikma has an even larger advantage over the next centre. A club with a stable backcourt should not pay for the abstract honour of signing the best player if the second-best fit improves it more.

That is why FA2 will be decided by sequence. Griffith should receive the broadest interest, but the first Bird or Sikma offer can force a club to choose between certainty now and a deeper guard pool later. The opening round proved teams will spend heavily on their own foundations. The next one will reveal who is willing to cross the aisle.
