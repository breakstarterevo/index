After FA2, Rickey Green Controls A Smaller Offseason Board

The stars have signed, leaving one proven lead guard, one young rebounder and a crowded group of specialists to define the second wave.

Nina Vale1984 Offseason - After FA2

The first wave changed the standard for this board. Alex English, Purvis Short, Greg Ballard, Kyle Macy and Artis Gilmore are no longer theoretical solutions; they are roster decisions already made. What remains is not a second star market. It is a fit market, and Rickey Green is the only available player with a clean claim to controlling a starting position.

Green's 124 overall rating is six points clear of the next name, but the gap matters because of role scarcity. Aston Villa has only two guards and nearly $32 million in room. Arsenal has one guard and $8.81 million to spend. AC Sparta Praha can afford to bid without treating him as a luxury. That is enough competition to make Green the market setter even if the market itself has shrunk.

The Post-FA2 Top Ten
RankPlayerMarket case
  1. 1Rickey Green — PG, 27

    A proven 124-overall lead guard in a market with several thin backcourts. He offers the best combination of certainty, positional leverage and immediate minutes.

  2. 2Larry Smith — PF, 24

    The best age-value play left. His 118 overall and 119 potential make him a dependable rebounder with more contract horizon than the veteran alternatives.

  3. 3Billy Knight — SF, 30

    The board's most credible remaining scoring wing. Age limits the term, but clubs with one-wing rosters should value the immediate offensive floor.

  4. 4Mike Dunleavy — SG, 28

    A stable veteran guard whose 113 overall profile fits teams that missed the first-wave playmakers and cannot wait on development.

  5. 5James Bailey — PF, 25

    The strongest upside case in the second tier: 112 now, 117 potential, and young enough to justify more than a one-year patch.

  6. 6Mike McGee — SG, 23

    Youth and scoring upside separate him from the veteran depth market. He is the swing worth taking for a club with minutes to offer.

  7. 7Al Wood — SF, 24

    A straightforward 110-overall wing solution. His value rises because Richmond, United, AC Sparta Praha and Ajax all show obvious wing scarcity.

  8. 8Mike O'Koren — PG, 24

    The younger fallback at point guard. He lacks Green's ceiling but can stabilize a rotation without forcing a club into a veteran-only timeline.

  9. 9Michael Wiley — SF, 25

    A prime-age wing with a clear bench role. The case is less about upside than avoiding a roster hole before camp.

  10. 10Kent Benson — C, 26

    The cleanest remaining center profile for a team needing functional size rather than a headline.

What Changes The Board

The second wave will be decided by whether Aston Villa uses its unmatched room to solve both guard and frontcourt depth, and whether Arsenal treats its one-guard roster as an emergency. If either club moves early, Green's leverage becomes a contract. If both wait, the market can flatten quickly.

After Green, price and role should matter more than rank. Smith and Bailey fit longer timelines. Knight and Dunleavy fit teams that need credible minutes immediately. McGee is the upside bet. The board is smaller now, but the consequences of choosing the wrong archetype are not.
