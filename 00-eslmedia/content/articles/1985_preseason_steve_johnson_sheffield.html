The Preseason Contradiction - Sheffield United

Steve Johnson Scored 40 A Night. Sheffield Lost Every Game.

The preseason's most outrageous individual performance belongs to a winless team, which makes Johnson's brilliance less comforting and Sheffield's construction impossible to excuse.

Damon Cross1985 Preseason - Lead Column

Steve Johnson averaged 39.5 points and 12.2 rebounds while shooting 58.3 percent. Sheffield United went 0-6. If those two sentences can sit beside each other without causing an emergency meeting, then what exactly qualifies as an emergency in Sheffield?

This was not one theatrical night inflating a small sample. Johnson played 43 minutes per game. He carried the scoring, cleaned the glass and still found enough breath for 3.3 assists. He gained ten overall points during the development cycle and arrived in preseason looking like a man who had discovered the league was using the wrong difficulty setting.

And his team lost every time.

Brilliance Became A Distress Signal

There are productive stars and there are emergency generators. Johnson became the second because Sheffield gave him no choice. The club was outscored by 14.8 points per game, the worst margin in ELB. Bimbo Coles led the team with 4.2 assists. Think about the geometry of that offence: one forward manufacturing nearly 40 points while the primary passing supply struggles to reach five assists.

A great scorer can rescue bad possessions. He cannot build an ecosystem by himself. When Johnson drew a second defender, Sheffield needed quick decisions, cutting and another player capable of turning the tilted floor into an advantage. Too often the reward for breaking the defence was another difficult shot arriving slightly later in the clock.

The defence was no kinder. Johnson's rebounding prevented some damage, but a forward collecting 12 boards is not proof that the team controlled the glass. It can be proof that opponents kept leaving work behind. Sheffield allowed 118.3 points per game. Johnson's offence was not chasing victory so much as chasing the leak.

Do Not Blame The Man Holding The Building Up

Somebody will look at the record and mutter about empty numbers. That accusation is lazy here. Empty production usually comes from inefficient volume, indifferent defence or statistics accumulated after the contest has been decided. Johnson shot 58.3 percent. He added 1.5 blocks per game. His production was not empty; the space around it was.

Could he pass earlier? Certainly. Could he conserve more energy for the defensive end? Possibly. But those are refinements for a team with alternatives. Sheffield's current setup asks Johnson to create the advantage, finish the advantage, recover the miss and then explain why the advantage did not win.

The best counterargument is that preseason is designed for stress-testing. Perhaps Sheffield intentionally extended Johnson, withheld combinations and accepted losses to discover where its roster breaks. Fine. It discovered the answer six times. The test is over. Keeping the same conditions when the regular season begins would no longer be experimentation. It would be negligence with a clipboard.

What Sheffield Must Change

First, somebody else must initiate. Coles does not need to become Maurice Cheeks overnight, but Sheffield needs an organiser who can enter the offence before Johnson has to wrestle it into existence. Earlier actions would let Johnson catch against a moving defence instead of greeting two set defenders above the block.

Second, the club needs a second scorer opponents fear enough to guard honestly. Johnson's 39.5 is thrilling until it becomes the plan. A healthy offence should be delighted when he reaches 30, not terrified when he does not reach 40.

Third, Sheffield must decide whether its bigs are screening partners, finishers or rebounders. At the moment their collective identity appears to be “present.” ELB will punish that ambiguity. Ajax has Cliff Robinson inside and Iguodala connecting the floor. Aston Villa can put Larry Nance and Xavier McDaniel into the same frontcourt problem. Marseille has Robert Parish owning the glass. Johnson cannot be Sheffield's answer to every one of those shapes.

There is also a minutes problem hiding inside the scoring headline. Forty-three minutes per game in preseason is not a badge of competitive seriousness. It is an admission that the staff did not trust the game without him. Johnson can handle a large regular-season workload, but using October to rehearse exhaustion is a strange way to protect the one advantage Sheffield knows it possesses.

A more functional rotation would let him sit without the offence losing its language. It would also preserve his legs for the defensive possessions that decide whether 35 points become a win. Sheffield does not need to reduce Johnson's importance. It needs to stop expressing that importance through overuse.

The Cruel Part

Johnson's development should have been joyful. He rose from 116 to 126 overall and then played beyond even that improvement. At 25, he appears ready for the largest role of his career. Most clubs spend years hoping a player will force this kind of recalibration.

Sheffield received the gift and immediately attached a burden to it. Every huge performance became evidence of how little help arrived. Every rebound reminded the club that its forward was cleaning up another job. Every loss turned praise into an interrogation.

Johnson did not put up empty numbers. Sheffield put an empty structure around real ones.

The season has not started, which is Sheffield's only comforting fact. Preseason records disappear. Rotations change. One acquisition or one coherent lineup can move responsibility back toward something humane.

But the warning could not be clearer. Johnson has already done the hard part. He became better, proved it against live opponents and produced at a level no one else matched. Sheffield must now build a team in which 39.5 points feels like an eruption instead of the minimum payment required to remain visible.

If the losses continue, do not ask whether Johnson can carry more. Ask why Sheffield keeps handing him things no player can carry.
