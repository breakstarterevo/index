January Tier 1 Power Rankings: Richmond Hold, Bayern Charge

AFC Richmond keep the Tier 1 lead on season-long form, but Bayern Munich's six-game run turns the top of the board into a real argument.

Nina ValeJanuary 1983

The January board starts with a distinction: best season profile is not identical to best month. Richmond still hold the strongest full resume, but Bayern produced the month that most changed the tier.

1. AFC Richmond - stayed level at No. 1

Richmond are 17-5 overall and went 6-2 in January, enough to keep the top spot. Magic Johnson remains the cleanest explanation for their stability. The caveat is that Crystal Palace beat them, and Bayern beat them by 13. The lead is earned, not untouchable.

2. Bayern Munich - up four spots

Bayern are the board's biggest riser. A 7-2 month, plus-81 differential and W6 finish move Bernard King's team from middle-tier respectability into direct pressure on Richmond. The 137-85 win over Atletico was the loudest result; the closing run was the better sign.

3. Sheffield United - stayed level

Sheffield United went 6-3 in January and sit 12-10 overall with a plus-93 season differential. Rickey Green's group lacks the glamour of the teams around them, but the results are stubborn. The L2 finish keeps them behind Bayern rather than alongside them.

4. Real Madrid - down three spots

Madrid's November case has cooled. Sidney Moncrief still gives them a high-grade two-way anchor, but a 3-5 January and losses to Crystal Palace, Benfica and Bayern make the drop unavoidable. The season differential is still positive; the trend is not.

5. AC Milan - stayed level

Milan remain the most confusing team in the tier. Larry Bird is still the highest-end player in any room, and the 123-93 win over Crystal Palace showed the ceiling. A 2-5 month and a 26-point home loss to Atletico showed the floor. Fifth is a compromise, not a compliment.

6. Atletico Madrid - up one spot

Atletico did not solve themselves, but 4-4 in January is enough to climb while others slid. James Donaldson's side beat Benfica by 35 and Milan by 26, then absorbed Bayern's 52-point hit. That volatility explains both the rise and the ceiling.

7. Benfica - down three spots

Moses Malone continues to make the individual case look better than the team case. Benfica went 2-5, lost by 35 to Atletico, and sit 8-15 overall. The win at Real Madrid prevents a complete collapse on the board, but not a fall.

8. Crystal Palace - stayed level

Palace beat Richmond and Real Madrid, which is why the bottom line is not completely hopeless. The larger profile is still 7-15 with a minus-166 season differential. Darrell Griffith gives Palace punch; the defense gives them too many problems.

Richmond keep first because the season says they should. Bayern are second because January says the gap is closing.

The next movement trigger is simple: Richmond need to beat the teams now chasing them, while Bayern need to prove January was not just one hot month arriving loudly.
