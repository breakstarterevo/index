November 1985 Tier 3 Power Rankings: Barcelona Turn One Place Into A Crowd

Barcelona have the lead and the margin, but one promotion place has made every challenger’s small flaw feel like a season-changing expense.

Nina ValeNovember 1985

Barcelona's first month contains a 52-point win, a four-game winning streak and the division's best point differential. It also contains a 101-85 loss at Tottenham. In a tier with one promotion place, that second sentence is why the first cannot yet end the race.

The trust board measures who is most likely to own that one place if November's habits continue. Margin separates Barcelona from Valencia; recent losses separate Valencia from the leader; preseason expectation explains several much larger falls below them.

Tier 3 Trust BoardNovember 1985
  1. 1BarcelonaUP 1 FROM NO. 2
    6-2 overall | 5-2 Sim 1 | +105 | W4 | Paul Pierce

    Barcelona beat Manchester United 134-82 and Manchester City 141-119, then kept winning. Paul Pierce and Bernard King give them two efficient scoring answers. Tottenham proved the offense can be compressed; nobody else sustained that solution.

  2. 2ValenciaUP 1 FROM NO. 3
    5-3 | 5-3 Sim 1 | +50 | L2 | Brian Shaw

    Valencia's 114-82 win at Juventus is the best direct evidence beneath Barcelona. Isiah Thomas is averaging 12.4 assists, and the offense travels. Two closing losses turned a possible lead into a one-game chase, making December less about invention than composure.

  3. 3JuventusUP 4 FROM NO. 7
    4-3 | 3-3 Sim 1 | +30 | L3 | Larry Smith

    The season profile is positive; the direction is not. Juventus conceded only 103 points per game overall and beat Manchester United by 23, then closed on three losses. Third reflects a defense worth trusting and a trajectory that cannot be ignored.

  4. 4Tottenham HotspurUP 2 FROM NO. 6
    4-4 | 4-3 Sim 1 | +9 | Hubert Davis

    Tottenham own the result every challenger wants: a 16-point win over Barcelona. Steve Kerr's shooting gives a modest offense a shortcut. The loss at Manchester United and an even overall record explain why one perfect matchup has not become a top-three file.

  5. 5MonacoDOWN 1 FROM NO. 4
    4-5 | 3-4 Sim 1 | -35 | W1 | Andre Drummond

    Andre Drummond's 26.1 points and 14.7 rebounds keep Monaco physically credible. A 23-point home loss to Arsenal exposes the complication: the interior advantage has not protected a defense giving back too many clean perimeter possessions.

  6. 6ArsenalDOWN 1 FROM NO. 5
    3-4 | 2-4 Sim 1 | -4 | W2 | Chris Bosh

    Sporting finished with two wins and Chris Bosh is scoring 30.4 points on 69.3 percent true shooting. The recovery deserves attention. It does not erase four early losses or the 139 points Valencia put on them.

  7. 7Manchester CityDOWN 6 FROM NO. 1
    3-5 | 3-4 Sim 1 | -24 | W1 | Luol Deng

    No preseason claim aged worse. City beat Sporting by 13 and finished the sim near level on margin, but Barcelona scored 141 in their building and three official wins cannot preserve a ranking built on games that did not count.

  8. 8Manchester UnitedSTAYED NO. 8
    2-5 | 2-4 Sim 1 | -131 | J.J. Redick

    United beat Tottenham, which is more than a bottom team usually offers. They also lost at Barcelona by 52 and own the league's most severe scoring deficit. One upset is a clue; minus-131 is the identity until further notice.

Control Team: Barcelona   Biggest Riser: Juventus   Trust Problem: Tottenham   Panic Team: Manchester City

Barcelona have made the standard visible. Valencia remain close enough to punish a stumble, Juventus must stop a bad run and Tottenham must prove their win over the leader travels. With one ticket available, nobody gets to call a warning temporary for very long.
